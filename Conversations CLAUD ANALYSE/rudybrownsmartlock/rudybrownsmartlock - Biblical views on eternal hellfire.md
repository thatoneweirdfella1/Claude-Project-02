# Biblical views on eternal hellfire

---

**ASSISTANT:**
 This is a topic where the Bible itself contains several different descriptions, and Christians have interpreted them in meaningfully different ways. Here's an overview:

## What the Bible actually says

**Passages suggesting eternal conscious torment:**
- Revelation 20:10 describes the devil being "tormented day and night forever and ever" in a lake of fire
- Matthew 25:46 contrasts "eternal punishment" with "eternal life"
- Mark 9:43-48 quotes Jesus describing hell as a place "where the worm does not die and the fire is not quenched"
- Revelation 14:11 mentions smoke rising "forever and ever" with "no rest day or night"

**Passages suggesting destruction/annihilation:**
- Matthew 10:28 says God can "destroy both soul and body in hell" — the word "destroy" suggests ending, not endless torment
- Romans 6:23 says "the wages of sin is death" — not eternal suffering
- 2 Thessalonians 1:9 says the wicked face "eternal destruction"
- Matthew 7:13 describes the broad road leading to "destruction"

**Passages suggesting possible restoration:**
- Colossians 1:19-20 says God will "reconcile all things" to himself
- 1 Corinthians 15:22 says "in Christ all shall be made alive"
- Philippians 2:10-11 says every knee will bow and every tongue confess

---

## The three main Christian views

1. **Eternal Conscious Torment** — the traditional view held by much of Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant Christianity. The unsaved suffer consciously forever.

2. **Annihilationism (Conditional Immortality)** — held by Seventh-day Adventists, Jehovah's Witnesses, and a growing number of evangelicals. The unsaved are ultimately destroyed rather than kept alive to suffer. The "eternal" refers to the *permanence* of the result, not the *duration* of suffering.

3. **Universal Reconciliation (Christian Universalism)** — a minority view held throughout church history. All souls are eventually restored to God. The fires of hell are *purifying* rather than punishing.

---

## A key interpretive question

The Greek word translated "eternal" (*aiónios*) is debated. It can mean "of the age to come" or relate to God's timeless nature — not necessarily "endless duration" in a simple sense. This debate is central to how scholars land on very different readings of the same texts.

So — the Bible does use fire imagery for hell, but whether that means literal endless burning is genuinely disputed, even among serious Bible scholars. It's one of the more contested questions in Christian theology.
