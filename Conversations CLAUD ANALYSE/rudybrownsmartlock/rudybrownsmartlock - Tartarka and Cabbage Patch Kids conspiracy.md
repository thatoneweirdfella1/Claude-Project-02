## Tartaria (or Tartary)

**What it is historically:** Tartary is a real historical name for Central Asia and Siberia, used for centuries by Western Europeans and Russians to refer to various regional ethnic groups and nomadic peoples in that area.

**The conspiracy theory:** The "Tartarian Empire" conspiracy asserts that Tartary was actually a lost civilization with advanced technology and culture — a theory that originated as pseudoscientific Russian nationalism, with aspects first appearing in Anatoly Fomenko's "New Chronology" in the mid-1970s.

The globalized, internet version of the theory — which really took off around 2016 — makes much bigger claims: believers hold that famous landmarks like the U.S. Capitol, the Taj Mahal, the Great Pyramids, and the Great Wall of China were all actually built by Tartarians, and that history was rewritten to make these buildings seem younger and more modern.

The "mud flood" is the central catastrophe: a series of "mud floods" supposedly wiped the Tartarian Empire off the face of the Earth, with some theorists claiming this happened as recently as 100 years ago. Believers point to buildings around the world that have windows and doorways partially submerged below current ground level as evidence.

On TikTok, the #tartaria tag has amassed almost 300 million views, and the associated #mudflood tag a further 150 million. Scholars have dubbed it "the QAnon of architecture."

## The Cabbage Patch Kids Conspiracy

This is where the theory gets especially baroque. It connects Tartaria to the real historical phenomenon of the **Orphan Trains** (1854–1929), during which more than 250,000 children were taken from orphanages, hospitals, and the streets and shipped across America — stripped of their names and severed from their pasts.

The Tartaria community's interpretation: these weren't orphans at all, but displaced children of Tartarian survivors. After mud floods, fires, and earthquakes destroyed much of the empire, and the Napoleonic and Civil Wars killed off many inhabitants, the surviving children were distributed to major towns and cities — not just as slave labor, but to repopulate.

The Cabbage Patch connection comes in two layers:

1. **Victorian postcards:** Theorists point to real antique postcards showing children emerging from or surrounded by cabbages. Some in the Tartaria community interpret these as evidence of orphaned Tartarian children, or possibly a repopulation and cloning program.

2. **The 1980s toy:** The Cabbage Patch Kids toy line, with its staged "births" from soil, legal "adoption" forms, and the stripping of family origin, is interpreted as having rehearsed the orphan ritual in millions of homes. The idea being that the toy's unusual marketing was not innocent — it was ritual reenactment, encoding the true story of Tartarian child displacement into popular culture.

The most prominent recent proponent is a British writer named Guy Anderson, whose book *Tesla & The Cabbage Patch Kids* ties it all together — arguing that after a cataclysmic reset involving mud floods, war, and coordinated events in the 18th century, millions of children were displaced from former Tartarian territories, and a repopulation program produced thousands of human clones who would grow up never knowing anything of the fallen empire. Nikola Tesla is woven in as someone who independently re-discovered Tartarian free energy technology.
