### WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWS

**DOCUMENTED — THE US FLEET AND WHAT JUST CHANGED:**

The Session 1 figure of "just over 11,000" for the US military drone fleet was the legacy number. That figure is now structurally obsolete. Here is what has happened since:

In June 2025, Trump signed an executive order titled "Unleashing American Drone Dominance." In July 2025, Secretary of War Pete Hegseth issued a follow-up memorandum. In December 2025, the Pentagon launched a $1.1 billion Drone Dominance Program with a stated goal of purchasing and equipping forces with over 300,000 domestically produced, weaponized small drones by 2027. The first phase ordered 30,000 one-way attack drones. Phase two, scheduled for August 2026, targets an additional 50,000–60,000.

Separately, the US Army's SkyFoundry program is targeting one million drones fielded within two to three years through public-private manufacturing partnerships.

The $150 billion defense reconciliation bill passed in spring 2025 includes $1 billion specifically for expanding the one-way attack drone industrial base, $50 million to accelerate autonomous targeting, and $1.1 billion for small UAS expansion.

The phrase "one-way attack drone" is the term the military uses for what Ukraine called "kamikaze drones" — expendable platforms that fly to a target and detonate. They cost hundreds to thousands of dollars each. They require no pilot. They require no recovery. They are produced at industrial scale and deployed in swarms.

**DOCUMENTED — RUSSIA:**

Russia produces approximately 5,500 drones per month. Ukraine produced an estimated 2.2 million drones in 2024. More than two-thirds of Russian tanks destroyed by Ukraine were taken out with first-person-view drones. This is the proof of concept the US military is scaling from — not theoretical future warfare, but an ongoing documented conflict demonstrating that mass-produced expendable autonomous systems defeat conventional armor at scale.

**DOCUMENTED — CHINA:**

China controls 70–80% of global drone production and manufacturing capacity. The US FY25 National Defense Authorization Act banned new foreign-manufactured drones from the US market specifically as a counter to Chinese dominance. China has developed loyal wingman UCAVs, stealth unmanned combat aircraft, and autonomous air-to-air capable platforms — with multiple new systems unveiled in August and September 2025.

**DOCUMENTED — THE LEGAL GAP:**

The UN General Assembly passed a resolution on Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS) in December 2024 with 166 votes in favor and 3 opposed. The resolution acknowledged a potential two-tiered approach — banning some LAWS while regulating others. However: this resolution is non-binding.

No binding international treaty governing LAWS exists. The CCW Group of Experts has been deliberating since 2014 — ten years of discussion with zero binding output.

The nations developing and deploying autonomous weapons — the US, Russia, China — are specifically the nations blocking binding restrictions. The US opposes a preemptive ban. Russia and China voted against the December 2024 UN resolution entirely.

**The accountability gap is confirmed by Human Rights Watch and peer-reviewed legal literature:** Autonomous weapons that rely on AI make targeting decisions through opaque "black box" processes. There are documented legal obstacles to holding individual operators criminally liable for the unpredictable actions of systems they cannot understand. The programmers and developers face civil law challenges. The result, named explicitly in HRW's 2025 report, is an "accountability gap" — a situation where civilian deaths can occur with no individual legally responsible.

**THE LAVENDER SYSTEM — DOCUMENTED, OPERATIONAL:**

The AI system called "Lavender," used by the Israeli military in Gaza, was reported by 972 Magazine and subsequently reviewed by multiple independent researchers. It directed airstrikes by identifying targets algorithmically. The system represents not hypothetical future capability — it is a documented operational deployment of AI-assisted targeting with accountability questions now before the ICC and international legal scholars.
