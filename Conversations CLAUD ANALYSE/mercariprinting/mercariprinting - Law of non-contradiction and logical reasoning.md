**ASSISTANT:**
 Yes, there are a few options that can do this without paying.

**CapCut** is probably your best bet for free. It handles split-screen layouts, you can manually sync clips by their timestamps, and it's available on both Android and iOS. The free version is genuinely capable for this kind of project without pushing you toward a paywall for the basic stuff you're describing.

**VN Video Editor** is another solid free option that does split-screen and gives you more precise timeline control, which matters when you're trying to sync two camera angles accurately.

**DaVinci Resolve** is the most powerful free option but it's desktop only, not mobile. If you're doing this on a computer it's overkill for what you need but it will absolutely do it perfectly, including frame-accurate timestamp syncing.

The honest answer on the timestamp syncing part though — none of these apps will *automatically* read the metadata timestamps from two different cameras and sync them for you. You'd have to do that part manually by lining up the clips on the timeline yourself. The way people usually handle it is by finding a common moment in both recordings, like a sound or a visible event that appears on both cameras, and using that as your sync point.

If your cameras both record audio, that's actually the easiest sync method — most editors let you line up the audio waveforms visually which is pretty quick.
