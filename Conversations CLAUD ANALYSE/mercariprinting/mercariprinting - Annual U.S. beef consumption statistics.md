It's called **Structured Analytical Techniques (SATs)** — the same methodology used by intelligence agencies to verify findings before they become actionable intelligence.

---

**The ones that matter most for your work:**

**1. Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH)**
Developed by CIA analyst Richards Heuer. You take every piece of evidence and run it against ALL possible explanations — not just the one you believe. The hypothesis that survives elimination is your strongest finding. It's specifically designed to defeat confirmation bias.

**2. Red Team Analysis**
You build the strongest possible case AGAINST your own findings. If your conclusion survives a genuine red team attack it's solid. Intelligence agencies literally hire separate teams whose only job is to destroy the primary team's conclusions.

**3. Key Assumptions Check**
List every assumption your conclusion rests on. Then attack each one individually. If one assumption collapses — does your whole conclusion collapse with it or does it still stand?

**4. Linchpin Analysis**
Identify the single most critical piece of evidence your entire case rests on. If that one thing is wrong — everything falls. If it holds under maximum scrutiny — everything holds.

---

**What investigative journalists specifically use:**

- **The Bradlee Standard** — named after Ben Bradlee of the Washington Post. Two completely independent sources confirming the same specific fact before it gets printed. Not two sources who heard it from the same place. Genuinely independent.

- **Document First** — every claim traced back to a primary document. Not a quote about a document. Not someone describing a document. The actual document.

- **The Hostile Witness Test** — can someone who is actively motivated to disprove your finding look at your evidence chain and still not be able to break it?
