Dead Sea Scrolls.


**ASSISTANT:**
 The Dead Sea Scrolls were written primarily in **Hebrew**, with a significant portion in **Aramaic**, and a smaller number in **Greek**.

Breaking it down:

**~80%** — Hebrew (mostly Biblical texts and sectarian documents)

**~15%** — Aramaic (including the Book of Enoch and the Genesis Apocryphon)

**~5%** — Greek (including fragments of the Septuagint)

The Hebrew used isn't modern Hebrew — it's **Biblical and Late Second Temple period Hebrew**, roughly 250 BCE to 70 CE. Think of it like finding documents written in Shakespearean English. Technically the same language. But good luck reading it at a glance.

---
**Septuagint** — the ancient Greek translation of the Hebrew Bible/Old Testament.
**Sectarian** — documents belonging to a specific religious group, in this case believed to be the Essenes.
