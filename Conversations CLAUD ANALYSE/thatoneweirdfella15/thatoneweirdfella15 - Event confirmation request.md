 There are a few common reasons people share content like this:

**They genuinely don't know it's satire.** Many satire sites deliberately mimic the look of breaking news. If someone sees it shared by a friend and doesn't check the source, they may believe it and pass it along in good faith.

**Confirmation bias.** If the content aligns with what someone already believes, they're less likely to scrutinize it. People who dislike Trump *want* this to be true, so they share it without verifying.

**Intentional manipulation.** Some people know it's false but share it anyway because it reinforces a narrative they want to spread. The emotional satisfaction of "owning" the other side can override the concern for accuracy.

**Engagement farming.** Outrage and dramatic headlines drive clicks, shares, and attention. Some accounts profit from this regardless of whether the content is true.

**The correction never catches up.** Even if the post gets debunked, the debunking rarely spreads as far as the original. The false impression lingers.

The broader problem is that satire sites occupy a legal gray area — they can claim "it's just a joke" while knowing full well many readers will take it literally. That makes it a fairly effective (and irresponsible) way to spread a political message without being held accountable for it.
