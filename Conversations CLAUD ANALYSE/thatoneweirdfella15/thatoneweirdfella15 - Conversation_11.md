**The separate mathematical model — norm change vs. regime change**

This is where the two thresholds diverge significantly. Chenoweth's 3.5% applies to **regime change**. A completely separate body of research covers **norm change**:

When a committed minority pushing change was below 25% of the total group, its efforts failed. But when the committed minority reached 25%, there was an abrupt change in group dynamics, and very quickly the majority of the population adopted the new norm. In one trial, a single person accounted for the difference between success and failure. This is Damon Centola's research published in *Science* in 2018.

Empirical social physics suggests committed minorities between 10–40% can flip social conventions, with 25% as a robust central estimate. The threshold is probabilistic and context-sensitive.

A third study found an even lower threshold for opinion shift: research by Xie et al. suggested the size of committed minorities needed to instigate rapid change in majority opinion was approximately 10% — when this percentage of committed agents who constantly advocate for an opposing opinion exceeds the threshold, the time taken for the entire population to adopt the committed opinion dramatically decreases.
