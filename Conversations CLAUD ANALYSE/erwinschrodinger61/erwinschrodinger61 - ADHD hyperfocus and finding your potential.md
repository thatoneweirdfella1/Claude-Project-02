**HUMAN:**
Why does certain lighting cause cameras to see these lines that move slowly like the camera is on the other side of blinds even though they arent


**ASSISTANT:**
 That's called the **rolling shutter effect** combined with **flickering light interference**.

Here's what's actually happening:

Most indoor lights — especially fluorescent and LED bulbs — don't actually stay on constantly. They flicker on and off at 50 or 60 times per second, synchronized to the electrical grid frequency. Your eye can't detect it because it's too fast. But cameras record by scanning the image line by line from top to bottom rather than capturing the whole frame instantly.

So by the time the camera scans from the top of the frame to the bottom, the light has already flickered on and off multiple times. Different horizontal lines of the image get captured at slightly different moments in the flicker cycle — some lines catch the light on, some catch it off. That creates those horizontal bands.

They appear to move slowly because the flicker frequency of your light and the frame rate of your camera are slightly out of sync — like two metronomes that are close but not identical. That slight mismatch is what makes the bands drift slowly up or down rather than staying still or disappearing.

**The fix if it bothers you:**

Change the camera's anti-flicker setting to match your region — **60Hz in the US**, 50Hz in Europe. Most Wyze cameras have this setting buried in the camera's advanced settings. Matching the camera's scan rate to your grid frequency makes the bands disappear because they sync up instead of drifting.
