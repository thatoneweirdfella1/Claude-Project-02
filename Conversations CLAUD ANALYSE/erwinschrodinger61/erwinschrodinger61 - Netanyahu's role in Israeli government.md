## Israel has two "leaders" — but they're not equal

**The President** (currently Isaac Herzog) is largely a **ceremonial figurehead**. He represents the state at official functions, signs laws, and serves as a symbol of national unity — but he holds very little actual governing power. It's similar to the role of a monarch in the UK or a president in Germany.

**The Prime Minister** (Benjamin Netanyahu) is the **real head of government** — the one who actually runs the country. He controls the cabinet, sets policy, commands the military, leads foreign policy, and drives legislation. All the major decisions you hear about — war strategy, diplomatic moves, domestic reforms — come from the Prime Minister's office.

## Why does Israel work this way?

Israel is a **parliamentary democracy**, which means:
- Citizens vote for **political parties**, not directly for a prime minister
- Whichever party (or coalition of parties) wins enough seats in the **Knesset** (Israel's parliament) gets to form a government
- The leader of that coalition becomes **Prime Minister**

Netanyahu leads the **Likud party**, and has built governing coalitions that have kept him in power for a long time — he's actually the **longest-serving Prime Minister in Israel's history**.

## In short:
- 🏛️ **President** = symbolic head of *state* (ceremonial)
- 🏛️ **Prime Minister** = actual head of *government* (real power)

Netanyahu's power comes entirely from his role as Prime Minister and his command of the parliamentary coalition — not from the presidency, which is why you'll rarely hear much about the president in the news.
