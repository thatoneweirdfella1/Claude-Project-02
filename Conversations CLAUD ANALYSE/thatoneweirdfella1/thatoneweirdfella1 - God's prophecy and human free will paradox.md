**What Different Theological Traditions Say**

**On Foreknowledge vs. Causation**
Most theologians distinguish between *knowing* something will happen and *causing* it to happen. The classic argument is that God exists outside of time and simply *sees* all events simultaneously — the way you might watch a parade from above, seeing the beginning and end at once, without causing any of it.

**On Free Will**
The dominant answer across Judaism, Christianity, and Islam is that humans still make *free choices*. God knowing you'll choose evil doesn't mean He made you choose it — the responsibility stays with the human actors.

**On the Self-Fulfilling Prophecy Problem**
This is where your question gets really sharp. If people *read the prophecy and then intentionally act to fulfill it*, that's different from free will playing out naturally. That's humans using scripture as a *blueprint*, which many theologians actually consider a **corruption or misuse** of prophecy.

---

**What the Combined Books Suggest**

Across Torah, the New Testament, and the Quran, a consistent theme emerges — prophecy was generally intended as a **warning**, not a script. The prophets were saying *"if things continue this way, here is where it leads"* — not necessarily *"this is a fixed sequence you must complete."*

The Book of Revelation in particular was almost certainly written as **coded political commentary** about Rome, meant to give hope to persecuted Christians — not as a literal step-by-step manual for the end of the world.

---

**The Deepest Tension**

You've actually landed on something that even serious theologians find uncomfortable — if God is truly omniscient and omnipotent, then evil existing at all *seems* to implicate Him in it. This is called the **Problem of Evil**, and no tradition has fully resolved it. The answers generally fall into:

- God *permits* evil because free will requires it
- Evil is necessary for the *contrast* that makes goodness meaningful
- Human understanding is too limited to see the full picture

---

**The Bottom Line**

Your instinct is philosophically sound. There *is* a loop built into the prophecy-fulfillment cycle, especially when humans act *intentionally* to trigger it. The most honest answer is that the texts themselves are in tension on this — and that tension may be the point, pushing readers toward humility rather than certainty.
