The f*** is the difference between a utility pump and a water pump and a sump pump and what's the f****** difference between all the pumps actually


**ASSISTANT:**
 Good question. Here's the actual breakdown:

**Sump pump** - sits in a pit in your floor, pulls water that's already flooding in around it. Submersible. Gravity fed. Useless for your setup.

**Utility pump** - general purpose transfer. Moves water from A to B. Usually no pressure switch, no self-priming, just raw flow. Think draining a flooded basement or emptying a pool. Not built for on-demand shower use.

**Booster pump** - assumes water is already flowing and just adds pressure. Needs incoming water pressure to work. Won't pull from a jug.

**Diaphragm pump** - what you need. Uses a pulsing membrane to pull water in and push it out. Self-priming means it can suck water up from a container next to it. Pressure switch means it turns on when you open the valve and off when you close it. Built for exactly your setup.

**Centrifugal pump** - spins an impeller to move water. Has to be primed manually or submerged. Won't self-prime from a sitting jug. Showed up as the DACO MIN last time.

**Peristaltic pump** - squeezes a tube to move liquid. Medical/lab/wine transfer use. Low flow, no pressure, wrong application entirely.

So the magic words for your search are always: diaphragm, self-priming, pressure switch. Everything else is a different tool for a different job.
