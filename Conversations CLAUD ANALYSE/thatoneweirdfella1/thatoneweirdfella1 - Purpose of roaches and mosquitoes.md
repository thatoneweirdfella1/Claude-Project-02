# Purpose of roaches and mosquitoes

*2026-06-09T04:21:22.853409Z*

---


**HUMAN:**
I know that every animal has its purpose and decompose everything but what is the purpose of roaches and mosquitoes


**ASSISTANT:**
 Both get a bad rap but they actually pull real weight.

**Roaches**

Decomposers. They break down decaying organic matter, dead plants, rotting wood, animal waste. That material gets cycled back into soil nutrients. Without decomposers doing that work, organic waste just piles up. They're also food for birds, lizards, frogs, and small mammals. Some species pollinate plants. Rain forest roaches specifically are critical to forest floor nutrient cycling.

**Mosquitoes**

This one surprises people. Males don't bite at all. They're pollinators. Blueberries, orchids, and several other plants depend partly on mosquito pollination. Females (the biters) are a massive food source. Fish, bats, birds, dragonflies, frogs, and spiders all eat them heavily. Bats especially rely on them. Some fish species time their breeding cycles around mosquito larvae availability. The larvae themselves filter microorganisms out of standing water, cleaning it.

The honest answer is both species are links in chains that would visibly break without them. The problem isn't their existence. It's that they thrive in environments humans create and disturbed ecosystems push their populations out of balance.

The ones causing mass death from malaria, dengue, and yellow fever are about four or five species out of roughly three thousand. The rest are just out there pollinating and getting eaten.
