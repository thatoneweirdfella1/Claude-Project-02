Here's a clear picture of where things actually stand, as of early 2026:

**Replicator Program**

The Replicator initiative, unveiled in August 2023, was a DoD effort to field thousands of uncrewed, all-domain attritable autonomous systems by August 2025. However, the exact number fielded is classified, but a Congressional Research Service report indicated that DoD had fielded only hundreds by the target date, not the "multiple thousands" originally intended.

The program has since been renamed. As of December 2025, it operates as the "Defense Autonomous Warfare Group" (DAWG) and is now focused on larger, longer-range attack drones.

**True "Human Out of the Loop" LAWS**

As of 2025, most military drones and robots are *not* truly autonomous in the full "human out of the loop" sense. The U.S. does not publicly disclose exact numbers of any fully autonomous lethal systems, and the Pentagon's FY2025 NDAA only recently required the Secretary of Defense to provide Congress with an annual report on approvals and deployments of lethal autonomous weapons — the first such report was due by December 31, 2025. Those reports are classified or not yet public.

**New Drone Dominance Program**

The Pentagon's new Drone Dominance Program plans to purchase more than 200,000 industry-made drones by 2027, with 30,000 to be delivered by July 2026. These are primarily small, cheap one-way attack drones — but they are not fully autonomous "out of the loop" systems by current policy.

**Bottom Line**

The U.S. military does **not** publicly release a count of fully autonomous, human-out-of-the-loop weapons. Current DoD policy under Directive 3000.09 requires human oversight for lethal force decisions, meaning true "LAWS" as strictly defined are not officially deployed. The Replicator program fielded **hundreds** (not thousands) of autonomous-capable systems as of mid-2025, with ambitions for far larger numbers in coming years. Exact figures remain classified.
