## The Real Framework: Ray Dalio's 6-Stage Big Cycle

This comes directly from Dalio's book *Principles for Dealing with the Changing World Order* (2021) and his LinkedIn/X posts. The progression from each order to the next typically follows the same sequence of steps across all empires studied.

---

### STAGE 1 — New Order Begins
A new order begins after a war or revolution. New leadership consolidates power, debts are restructured or reduced, wealth gaps narrow, and conflicts subside. Think: USA post-WWII (1945), or the Dutch Republic after the Thirty Years War. The slate is wiped. People are motivated to rebuild.

---

### STAGE 2 — Building & Consolidation
There is further consolidation of power and the building of resource allocation systems. Strong institutions form. Education is prioritized. The middle class grows. Innovation is embraced, financial markets develop, and the empire gains a larger share of world trade. This is the period of genuine strength-building.

---

### STAGE 3 — Peace & Prosperity (The Peak)
There is peace and prosperity. Stage 3 is the high point in the Big Cycle — there is a lot of peace and prosperity. The empire is at or near its peak. The currency becomes the world reserve currency. Military is dominant. This is the golden era people look back on nostalgically. For the US, this roughly corresponds to 1950s–1990s.

---

### STAGE 4 — Excess & Decay Begin
There are great excesses in spending and debt and a widening of the wealth and political gaps. The forces that bred innovation, creativity, and growth fade. A rival power emerges. Government leadership may become corrupted and overconfident. Debt snowballs, and the central bank is forced to print money. The currency devalues. Wealth gaps grow, undermining productivity. People still feel wealthy but the foundation is rotting.

---

### STAGE 5 — Crisis & Intense Conflict (WHERE THE US IS NOW)
Stage 5 is when there are bad financial conditions and intense conflict. Dalio calls this the "pre-breakdown phase" characterized by bad financial conditions and internal conflict. Key issues include rising political polarization, significant economic inequality, and a loss of trust in institutions. The conflicts in the late part of Stage 5 typically build up to a crescendo that triggers violent fighting.

**As of early 2026:** Dalio has now claimed the U.S. has entered Stage 6 — the violent finale of order collapse, characterized by revolution and WWII-like conditions — citing the immigration crackdown, skyrocketing national debt, and breakdown of the rules-based international order.

---

### STAGE 6 — Civil War, Revolution, or Collapse
Stage 6 is when there are civil wars and revolutions, which leads back to Stage 1, and the whole cycle begins again. When the system to resolve disputes stops working, Stage 5 becomes Stage 6. When the system stops working for the majority, a revolution ensues and changes the system. This is the violent reset — the period that history records as a collapse. It ends when a new group wins, consolidates power, and starts Stage 1 again.

### FIRST: Understand what you're actually fighting

The problem is not ignorance. Studies show that 96% of people across 40 countries express environmental concern — yet only 65% of those concerned people report any willingness to take action. The gap between knowing and doing is not filled by more information. It is caused by something specific.

Learned helplessness occurs when an individual continuously faces negative, uncontrollable situations and stops trying to change their circumstances — even when they have the ability to do so. It manifests as lack of motivation, emotional numbness, and the conviction that nothing they do matters. This is the default state of most Americans regarding large systemic threats. When a culture or political identity repeatedly fails to achieve desired goals, perceptions of collective ability suffer — leading to fewer and fewer people even trying.

More information alone does not fix this. It often makes it worse. Continuously being exposed to problem-focused messages leads to learned helplessness and reduces people's willingness to engage in action. Standard reporting — problem-focused, doom-heavy — increases climate change awareness but simultaneously increases helplessness, creating a person who understands the danger but feels incapable of responding.
