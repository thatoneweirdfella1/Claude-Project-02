**ASSISTANT:**
 That's actually a really well-known phenomenon in Hollywood and there are a few things working together to cause it.

**The script is everything.**

The actors didn't get worse. The writing got worse. Great actors look bad in bad scripts because the dialogue gives them nothing to work with. Will Smith in part one had lines with personality. Everybody in part two was handed exposition and clichés. Even Jeff Goldblum, who is naturally charismatic, had almost nothing interesting to say.

**The "lightning in a bottle" problem.**

Part one nobody expected to be a hit. At the start of 1996 it was on nobody's radar, so there was no pressure to manufacture a cultural moment. That freedom often produces better work. Part two had twenty years of expectation sitting on it. When people are trying to recreate a feeling instead of just making a good movie, it almost always comes out stiff and hollow.

**Too many cooks.**

Two writers made part one. Five writers made part two. That almost never ends well. When a script goes through that many hands it loses a consistent voice. Characters stop feeling like people and start feeling like plot functions.

**The new cast had nothing to anchor to.**

Too much of the plot was dominated by younger actors who registered zero charisma, and that's partly because the script gave them nothing to build on. The original cast had full arcs. The new cast was basically handed the same archetypes with no room to develop them.

The returning actors actually weren't that bad in part two. They just looked worse by comparison because they were surrounded by weak material and underdeveloped new characters.

So the short version is: same actors, same director, completely different quality of raw material to work with. Garbage in, garbage out.
