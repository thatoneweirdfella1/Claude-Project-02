 That's a potential fire hazard worth taking seriously right now.

What you're describing sounds like **resistance heating** in the cord itself, which happens when:

**The cord is undersized for the load being drawn through it.** If the device on the other end is pulling more amperage than the wire gauge can handle, the entire length heats up, not just near the source. The hottest section being at the far end (where it connects to the power-drawing cord) points to that junction as the stress point, possibly a loose connection or a gauge mismatch between the two cords.

**What to unplug right now:** Both cords. Don't just unplug one and leave the other. If there's heat buildup in the insulation, continued use risks melting, arcing, or fire even after you walk away.

**What to check after it cools:**

Look at the cord gauge printed on the insulation (something like "18 AWG" or "16 AWG"). Thinner wire (higher number) handles less amperage. If you're daisy-chaining extension cords or using a light-duty cord to power something heavy like a space heater, AC unit, or multiple devices, that's likely the culprit.

Also check the connection point between the two cords for any discoloration, melted plastic, or burn smell.

**The rule of thumb:** Extension cords are temporary. Daisy-chaining them is a known fire risk. If you need more reach or capacity, a heavier gauge single cord or a dedicated outlet is the fix.
