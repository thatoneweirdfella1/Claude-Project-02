**ASSISTANT:**
 Unions are not exclusively a Democrat thing, though the association is strong and largely earned.

The AFL-CIO and most major unions have endorsed Democratic candidates for decades. Union households vote Democrat at significantly higher rates than non-union households. A lot of union leadership is deeply tied to the Democratic Party apparatus.

But the rank and file is messier. A big chunk of union members, especially in trades like construction, mining, and manufacturing, vote Republican. Trump made real inroads with union workers in 2016 and 2020, winning union households at rates that would have been unthinkable for a Republican 30 years ago.

Historically, unions were the backbone of the New Deal coalition and stayed loyal to Democrats through the mid-20th century. That alignment started fracturing in the 70s and 80s, accelerated by Reagan firing the air traffic controllers in 1981, and has been complicated ever since.

So: the institutions lean Democrat hard. The workers inside them are all over the map.

**ASSISTANT:**
 Yeah pretty much. That's a pretty strong indicator.

A few things in that picture point union:

Flying someone in from another state means they're pulling from a hall. Union halls dispatch workers regionally and nationally, especially for big commercial or industrial jobs. Non-union contractors almost never do that.

The physical is standard union protocol. Most trades require a pre-hire physical, sometimes a drug screen too, before you dispatch. It's baked into their process.

The combination of both together is a pretty clear tell. That's dispatch language, not a regular job posting.

Which union would depend on the work. Carpenters would be UBC (United Brotherhood of Carpenters). But if it's formwork, framing, or finish on a big commercial site that's almost certainly them.
