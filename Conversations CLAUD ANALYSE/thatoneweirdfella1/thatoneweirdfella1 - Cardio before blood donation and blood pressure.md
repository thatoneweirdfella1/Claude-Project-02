# Cardio before blood donation and blood pressure

*2026-06-01T09:53:55.218267Z*

---


**HUMAN:**
Does doing cardio before donating help your blood pressure? A roommate of mine says he has high blood pressure so they often times, most every time, denied him for blood pressure being too high trying to donate plasma. This stopped somewhat once he began doing blood pressure medications. He just ran out and has to donate in the morning and says he just needs to do cardio and I keep arguing that's how you raise your blood pressure. He swears by it and even says you have to take a break after doing it but it works. This would suggest that doing cardio raises your blood pressure but upon a break, lowers it more than it was before the cardio. Is this a thing?


**ASSISTANT:**
 Yeah, your roommate is actually onto something real, and you're both partially right.

You're correct that cardio raises blood pressure during exercise. That part is not debatable. But what he's describing is post-exercise hypotension, which is a documented physiological response.

Here's what happens: during aerobic exercise your blood pressure spikes, sometimes significantly. But after you stop, your cardiovascular system overcompensates. Blood vessels dilate, heart rate drops, and blood pressure can fall below your pre-exercise baseline for anywhere from thirty minutes to several hours. Studies have documented drops of around five to seven millimeters of mercury systolic, sometimes more in people who already have hypertension.

So the sequence he's describing is correct in principle. Run hard, rest, show up. The question is whether the timing and the drop are enough to clear the donation threshold, and that part is not guaranteed and not consistent across individuals. For someone with genuinely uncontrolled hypertension the baseline is higher, so the post-exercise dip might still land above the cutoff.

The other thing worth knowing is that donation centers are also checking your resting state when you walk in. If he's still slightly elevated from physical exertion or even just the stress of rushing to get there, it can work against him.

So it is a real mechanism. It's not guaranteed to work. And without his medication it's a coin flip at best depending on where his baseline actually sits right now.
