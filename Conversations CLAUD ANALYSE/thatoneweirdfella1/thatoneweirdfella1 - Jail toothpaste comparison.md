**ASSISTANT:**
 Sodium monofluorophosphate (MFP) is fluoride bonded to a phosphate molecule instead of delivered directly.

**How it works:**
Enzymes in saliva have to cleave the fluoride off the phosphate carrier before it can actually interact with tooth enamel. Extra step. Dependent on enzymatic activity to release the active component.

**vs. Sodium fluoride (in Sensodyne):**
Sodium fluoride dissociates immediately in water. Fluoride ion is available right away. More direct. More predictable uptake into enamel.

**Why MFP exists:**
It was developed earlier. Cheaper to produce. Historically used in Colgate and other mass market pastes. It works. Just not as efficiently.

**The institutional angle:**
MFP meets the FDA anticavity standard. That's all the jail paste needs to do. Check the box. Pass inspection. Doesn't need to be optimal.

**One other thing worth knowing:**
MFP has been around since the 1960s. The patents are long expired. It's commodity fluoride at this point. Pennies per gram.

Sensodyne is using the more bioavailable form AND adding potassium nitrate for nerve desensitization on top of it. Completely different tier of formulation.

Bottom line: MFP works but it's the budget route. Institutions buy it because it satisfies the legal requirement cheaply.
