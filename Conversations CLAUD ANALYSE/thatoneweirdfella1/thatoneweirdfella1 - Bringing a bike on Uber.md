The short answer: there's no dedicated Uber tier specifically for bikes, but here's what actually works.

**Uber XL** is your best bet. Uber XL vehicles tend to be SUVs or vans, meaning the driver has more space and is generally more comfortable with you loading a bike into the back. It's the most practical option available.

A few things to know going in:

Uber's policy on bringing a full-size bike is ultimately at the driver's discretion. Riders should ensure the bike can be disassembled or folded for transport, as standard cars typically don't have room for a full-size bike.

Some drivers, especially those driving Uber XL, have their cars fitted with bike racks, but they're under no obligation to have one, and there's no indication in the app whether a particular driver has a rack or not. There's currently no way to specifically request an Uber with a bike rack.

The practical move: book Uber XL, then message the driver before they arrive to give them a heads up. Gives them a chance to confirm or cancel rather than showing up and creating an awkward situation at the curb.

 Generally, the cheapest Uber times are:

**By time of day:**
Early mornings (5–7 AM) and mid-morning to early afternoon (10 AM–3 PM) on weekdays tend to have the lowest surge pricing since demand is lower.

**By day of week:**
Tuesday through Thursday are typically the cheapest days. Weekends, especially Friday and Saturday nights, are the most expensive.

**Times to avoid:**
Rush hours (7–9 AM and 4–7 PM), bar close (1–3 AM on weekends), major events, bad weather, and holidays all trigger heavy surge pricing.

**A practical tip:** If you see surge pricing, wait 10–15 minutes and check again. Surge tends to drop quickly once the spike in demand passes. You can also turn on price alerts in the app for a specific route.
