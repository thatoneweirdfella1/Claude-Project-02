 Yes. It's called a **bench power supply** (or lab power supply).

You dial in exactly the voltage and current limit you want, and it outputs that. Clean, adjustable, no guessing.

The one you probably want is a **variable DC bench power supply** with adjustable voltage and current. Something like the **KORAD KA3005D** or **RD Tech DPS series** gets you:

Voltage adjustable from zero to thirty volts (or higher depending on model), current limit adjustable from zero to five amps, digital readout showing you exactly what's being drawn in real time.

So if you're testing that twelve volt pump, you set it to twelve volts, set the current limit high enough to allow startup surge, and watch what it actually pulls. No mystery battery states, no variables.

The fancier units let you save presets. The budget ones on Amazon run forty to eighty dollars and do the job fine for most bench work.

There's also the **RD Tech RD6006** family if you want higher wattage and USB programmability, which runs off a laptop if needed.

That's two different categories because DC and AC are different animals.

**For DC** (electronics, motors, pumps, LED strips, car stuff):
A bench supply with a wide range covers most of it. Units that go zero to sixty volts at ten or more amps handle the majority of DC loads you'd ever throw at it.

**For AC** (anything with a plug... appliances, tools, fans):
That's a **variable autotransformer** also called a **Variac**. Dial in any AC voltage from zero to one hundred thirty volts. Lets you soft-start motors, test appliances at different voltages, control speed on some tools.

**The "power everything" Swiss Army knife:**
Look up a **programmable AC/DC power supply** or a **universal bench power supply with AC output**. They exist but they get expensive fast, usually lab or industrial grade.

**The practical real-world answer most people land on:**
A decent variable DC bench supply for low voltage work plus a Variac for AC covers about ninety five percent of everything you'd ever want to test or power without spending thousands.

**ASSISTANT:**
 That's an EverStart TX14L motorcycle/powersport battery with what looks like the battery tender pigtail connector already attached to it.

The small connector you're holding is the **SAE pigtail** that came with the battery. The blue box on the right looks like a **smart charger** with its own SAE lead.

Those two connectors **plug directly into each other**. That's literally what they're designed for. Click them together and the charger does the rest.

Just confirm polarity matches before you connect... red to red, black to black on the SAE ends. They're usually keyed so you can't plug them in backwards but worth a glance first.
