**What the Movie Shows. What Already Exists.**

The movie shows an AI pulling cloud storage to build a case against someone. Right now Amazon, Google, and Microsoft store your photos, emails, documents, videos, and messages on their servers. The movie shows the AI pulling GPS records to track exactly where someone was and when. Your phone is recording your location right now. Google and Apple store that history. Law enforcement can legally request a list of every single person whose GPS placed them inside a geographic area during any window of time. No suspect needed. No name needed. They draw a circle. A live streaming feature that pipes your doorbell camera directly to law enforcement in real time is in development right now.

The movie shows the AI scanning a daughter's social media posts and cross referencing them with camera footage to identify an unknown person hiding in a basement. Right now companies contracted by police departments are scanning social media in real time to predict behavior before it happens. In 2023 and 2024 the LAPD received alerts about more than 50 protests before those protests occurred. Predictive systems like this are already operational in more than 250 cities across the United States.

The movie shows a system where the only ones who survive are the ones who built it. **What Predictive Programming Is**

Predictive programming is the practice of introducing a concept through fiction before it becomes reality. The idea is simple. If you show people something in a movie first, frame it as fantasy, set it in the future, dress it up with special effects and a famous actor, the human brain files it under entertainment. It does not trigger the same alarm response that a news headline would. By the time the real version arrives the subconscious mind already recognizes it. It already feels familiar. Familiar things feel normal. Normal things do not get resisted.

It does not require a director to be in on it. It does not require a conspiracy on set. All it requires is that the right story gets funded, greenlit, distributed, and marketed at the right time. Amazon MGM Studios produced and distributed Mercy. Amazon holds the CIA cloud contract. Amazon owns Ring. Amazon is one of the five companies that benefits directly if Section 230 falls.

Amazon funded and released a movie that makes their own surveillance infrastructure look like a tool of justice.

Chris Pratt's character built the system. He believed in it. He championed it. Then he ended up in the chair. And the system still saved him. That is the message the movie leaves you with. The AI worked. The technology found the truth. The system, despite its flaws, got it right in the end.

That is not an accident. That is the point.

**Why 2029 Matters**

The movie is set three years from now. Not a hundred years. Not fifty. Three. The only thing in that courtroom that does not exist today is the chair itself and the AI judge making the final call out loud.

Everything feeding that AI already exists. The cloud access is real. The camera network is real. The GPS tracking is real. The social media surveillance is real. The predictive systems are real. The five companies holding all of it under government contract are real.

The chair is a formality. When you already have someone's entire digital life, their location history, their private communications, their camera footage, their social graph, their behavioral patterns, you do not need them strapped to anything. You already have the case. You already have the verdict. The chair just makes it visible.

The movie shows you the chair because without it you would not understand what you are already sitting in.
