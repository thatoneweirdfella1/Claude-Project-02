**ASSISTANT:**
 They never called themselves Nazis. That's just a fact.

The official name was the Nationalsozialistische Deutsche Arbeiterpartei, the NSDAP. That's what they called themselves. "Nazi" was external slang, used by opponents and critics, derived from the first syllable chunk of "Nationalsozialistische."

There are actually two competing theories on where the slang originated. The first is the straightforward abbreviation explanation -- "Nati" got clipped and softened into "Nazi," the same way Germans were already using "Sozi" as slang for Socialists. The second theory is more pointed: that it was a deliberate derogatory slur tied to rumors that Hitler had Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry, making "Nazi" a taunt aimed at the thing he'd hate most to be associated with. Two competing origin stories is exactly what you'd expect when the actual coinage isn't documented.

Either way, both explanations agree on the same thing: it was an insult from the outside. Not a self-label. The people in that party called themselves National Socialists or NSDAP members. The word "Nazi" was weaponized against them by their critics, which is why it stuck as the universal term after the war -- the rest of the world was the one using it.
