## HOW TO SPOT FAKE REVIEWS - NUMBERED FOR YOUR ADHD BRAIN

**1. Check the reviewer profile**
- Generic names (John Smith, Jane Doe) or random letters/numbers
- No profile picture or stock photo
- Account created recently but already has tons of reviews
- Reviews products across wildly different categories (power tools + collagen + pet toys = red flag)
- Only extreme ratings (all 5-star or all 1-star)

**2. Look for copy-paste phrases**
These are the dead giveaways:
- "I was skeptical but..."
- "Game-changer"
- "As someone who..."
- Generic praise with no specific details
- Overly enthusiastic language that sounds like an infomercial

**3. Check the timing**
- Bunch of reviews posted within 24-48 hours after weeks of silence
- Multiple reviews with similar timestamps
- Long gaps between "bursts" of positive reviews

**4. Read the actual content**
- **Fake reviews**: Set the scene ("my husband and I went on vacation...") without describing the actual product/service
- **Real reviews**: Include concrete details ("the bathroom was small," "check-in took 15 minutes," "the zipper broke after 2 weeks")
- Too many first-person pronouns ("I," "me," "my") - trying too hard to sound trustworthy
- More verbs than nouns (fake reviews focus on actions/feelings, not specifics)

**5. Look for "Verified Purchase" badges**
- On Amazon and other platforms, verified purchases are much more trustworthy
- Non-verified reviews are way more likely to be fake

**6. Check if reviewer got a free product**
- Some platforms show if the product was received for free in exchange for a review
- Not always fake, but biased

**7. Grammar and spelling**
- Content farms in other countries write many fake reviews
- Poor grammar, unnatural phrasing, or overly formal language are red flags

**8. Photo analysis**
- Stock photos or AI-generated images
- Multiple reviews using similar staged photos
- Photos that don't match the product description
