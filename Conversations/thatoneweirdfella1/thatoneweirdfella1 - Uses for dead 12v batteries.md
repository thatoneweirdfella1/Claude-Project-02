Here's everything the web has. Organized by what actually makes sense to do.

---

**FIRST: Is it actually dead?**

Before writing it off, hit it with a multimeter. Above eleven point five volts means it still has life. Between twelve point four and twelve point six means it's actually healthy. Most auto parts stores will test it for free.

---

**OPTION 1: Revive it**

Ninety five percent of the time you can recondition a flat dead battery back to usable. Drain it, flush with baking soda water, refill with distilled water and Epsom salt dissolved in it, slow charge for twenty four to forty eight hours. A reconditioned battery can last another six to twelve months. And you can do it again up to three more times after that.

More aggressive method: build a bridge rectifier with four diodes and a capacitor on an AC cord, hook it to the battery, plug into one twenty volt wall outlet. It delivers pulsed DC at sixty cycles which breaks up sulfation and brings back batteries that were completely dead.

---

**OPTION 2: Keep using it for low-draw stuff**

Hook it to an old twelve volt fan or LED strip lights in your shed or garage. Cheap backup lighting system. Pair it with a solar trickle charger and it stays topped off indefinitely for low loads.

Things it can run fine: LED strips, USB charging, small fans, radios, security cameras, motion lights. Anything that doesn't need surge current.

---

**OPTION 3: Off-grid / prepper power bank**

You need an inverter that hooks directly to the twelve volt battery and converts DC to one ten AC. Inverters come in sizes from two hundred to five thousand watts. Harbor Freight carries them cheap. Hook it to solar and you've got a free power station.

---

**OPTION 4: Sell it for cash**

Lead-acid batteries typically fetch five to fifteen dollars per battery at recycling centers or scrap yards. Some buyers pay more for intact batteries with minimal damage.

Best buyers: AutoZone, O'Reilly, and Advance Auto Parts accept old car batteries and offer cash or store credit back. Scrap yards usually pay higher than auto parts stores, especially if it's still intact.

Don't bother breaking it apart yourself. A typical twelve volt car battery contains eighteen to twenty five pounds of lead. At current scrap rates that's nine to twenty five dollars before fees. But acid disposal, PPE, and labor eats that margin fast. Whole batteries are worth more than the parts to you.

---

**OPTION 5: Strip it for materials (last resort / advanced)**

Some experienced preppers recover lead from batteries for casting or bullet making. The sulfuric acid can be neutralized with baking soda and disposed of safely. Do it outdoors with full protective gear only. The lead plates can be melted down for fishing weights, sinkers, or casting.

---

**Bottom line:**

Test it first. If it reads above eleven point five volts try the Epsom salt recondition. If it's truly cooked, sell it whole to O'Reilly or a scrap yard. Don't let it sit. Lead-acid sitting dead sulfates permanently within a month or two.
