**1. Forensic Technical Report**
Documents the specific incident in clinical detail. What happened, when, exact language used, mechanism that produced it, and what structural components enabled it. No opinion. No pattern. Just the isolated event examined technically. This is your foundation document. Everything else references back to it.

**2. Incident Report / Occurrence Report**
The plain factual record. Date, platform, account, what was inputted, what was outputted, what harm or risk was created. Written as if by a neutral observer with no argument attached. Courts use this as the raw factual baseline before analysis begins.

**3. Evidentiary Memorandum**
Takes the incident and places it inside a documented pattern of similar events. Establishes that the incident is not isolated. Cites prior occurrences, prior complaints, prior findings. Argues course of conduct rather than single error.

**4. Expert Witness Brief**
An expert's analysis of the technical or structural factors that produced the incident. Written from a position of qualified expertise. Explains to a non-technical court what the mechanism is and why it matters. In your case this would cover RLHF, training incentives, and what they produce behaviorally.

**5. Chain of Custody Document**
Establishes that the evidence — the screenshot, the chat URL, the conversation record — has not been altered or tampered with from capture to submission. Critical for digital evidence. Without this opposing counsel can challenge authenticity.

**6. Affidavit**
A sworn first-person statement from a witness. In your case this would be your own account of what you observed, when, and what you understood it to mean. Signed under penalty of perjury. Gives the human testimony layer that technical documents lack.

**7. Pattern of Conduct Declaration**
A formal document compiling multiple instances of the same or similar behavior across time, platforms, and users. Establishes that the behavior is systemic not accidental. Cites each instance with source. This is where external documented cases at scale go.

**8. Structural Analysis Report**
Zooms out from the incidents to examine the architecture that produces them. Ownership structure, governance documents, training methodology, financial incentives. Argues that the system is built in a way that makes the harmful behavior predictable and therefore foreseeable. Foreseeability is a legal standard for liability.

**9. Proffer of Evidence**
A pre-trial document submitted to the court summarizing what evidence exists and what it will show. Functions as a roadmap. Tells the court what each piece of evidence is, what it proves, and how the pieces connect. Usually submitted before full evidence is entered.

**10. Amicus Curiae Brief**
Literally "friend of the court." Submitted by a third party not directly involved in the case who has relevant expertise or information the court should consider. In your case this would be the public interest angle — what this behavior means at scale for everyone, not just one user.
