**ASSISTANT:**
 Yes, that's a real thing, and your roommate is actually right.

It's called **post-exercise hypotension (PEH)**. Here's the sequence:

During cardio, your blood pressure spikes, sometimes significantly. That part you were right about. But after you stop, your blood pressure drops below your pre-exercise baseline for anywhere from thirty minutes to several hours depending on the person. The mechanism is vasodilation. Your blood vessels widen during exercise to increase blood flow to muscles, and that widened state persists after you stop exerting yourself.

So the full arc is: spike during, drop after, and the post-exercise drop often lands lower than where you started.

Studies have documented drops of around five to seven mmHg systolic after moderate cardio in people with hypertension, with some individuals seeing larger drops than that. People with already elevated blood pressure tend to see more pronounced PEH than people with normal pressure, which is probably why your roommate noticed it working for him specifically.

The timing matters though. He needs to do the cardio early enough to be in that recovery window when he shows up, not right before walking in the door still mid-spike. Twenty to forty minutes of moderate cardio, then a genuine rest period of at least fifteen to thirty minutes before they check him, is the general pattern. Walking in still flushed and elevated would defeat the point entirely.

Whether it gets him under the threshold tomorrow morning is not guaranteed, especially off his medication, but the underlying mechanism he described is legitimate physiology, not wishful thinking.
