 Yeah both "proofs" fall apart the second you apply any actual logic.

The tourist argument assumes time travelers would just... show up everywhere casually. No regulation. No secrecy. No mission parameters. Just vibes and sightseeing. Every serious framework for how time travel would actually work assumes the opposite. You'd have strict protocols, limited windows, specific objectives. The absence of visible tourists proves nothing except that if it exists, it's controlled.

The party is even worse. He's essentially saying "I set a trap that only works if time travelers want to be caught, have no mission restrictions, are allowed to alter timelines freely, happened to receive my specific invitation across however many years or centuries, and decided a Cambridge dinner party was worth blowing their cover for." That's not an experiment. That's a wishlist.
