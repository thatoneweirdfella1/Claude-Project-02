Yes, you were right — it's called **Replicator**. The Pentagon announced it in August 2023, with the goal of cranking out "multiple thousands" of "attritable autonomous systems" across multiple domains within two years.

It was announced by Deputy Secretary of Defense Kathleen Hicks, who framed it as a response to China's military advantage — specifically its sheer mass of ships, missiles, and personnel. The idea was to counter that with swarms of systems that are "small, smart, cheap, and many."

Specific systems selected included AeroVironment's Switchblade 600 loitering munition, Anduril's Altius-600 and Ghost-X drones, and various uncrewed surface and undersea vehicles.

It didn't fully hit its goals though. Only "hundreds" rather than "thousands" of systems were delivered by the August 2025 target date, with critical technical issues and unclear costs.

As of late 2025, the program was renamed the **Defense Autonomous Warfare Group (DAWG)** and moved under Special Operations Command, now focusing on larger, longer-ranged attack drones.

**The Three Categories (Human Rights Watch classification)**
- **Human-in-the-loop** — weapon selects targets but only fires on human command. **Human-on-the-loop** — selects and attacks targets freely, but a human can intervene to stop it. **Human-out-of-the-loop** — selects targets and fires completely without human input or interaction

- Command responsibility — meaning who is legally liable for a kill — currently only applies to human-in-the-loop and human-on-the-loop systems. For fully autonomous systems, **nobody is legally responsible**
