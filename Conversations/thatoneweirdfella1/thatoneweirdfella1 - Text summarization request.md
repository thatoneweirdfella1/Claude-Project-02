**THE STRUCTURAL PIPELINE**
*How Legislation, Funding Design, and Institutional Failure Created a System Where Children Disappear*

**Key Findings**

$9.3 billion annually incentivizes child removal and adoption. $172 million (under 2%) funds family preservation. 34,800 missing foster children were never reported to authorities. 88% of child sex trafficking victims identified as runaways were in state care when they went missing. 60% of trafficking victims recovered in FBI raids came from foster care or group homes.

**The Legislation (ASFA, 1997)**

The Adoption and Safe Families Act created $4,000-$6,000 bonuses per adoption with no comparable incentive for family preservation. States that missed adoption quotas risked losing federal funding. By 1999, parental rights terminations in Chicago alone tripled. States collected $424 million in adoption bonuses by 2014. Academic review concluded the law "disrupted families permanently" while failing to move children from foster care to adoptive homes.

**The Funding Gap**

FY2023 breakdown: $5.1B for foster care, $4.3B for adoption and guardianship, $172M for prevention. That's a 54-to-1 ratio of removal/adoption funding to family preservation. 45 of 50 states spend less than 2% of their budget on child welfare. Funding and caseloads have been moving in opposite directions since 1998.

**The Missing Children**

A federal HHS audit found 46 states failed to report an estimated 34,800 missing foster children to NCMEC as required by law. Average age: 15. These weren't technical failures. Forty-six states, simultaneously and consistently, failed to report tens of thousands of missing children.

**The Trafficking Pipeline**

88% of child sex trafficking victims identified as runaways had been in state care. 60% of victims recovered in FBI raids came from foster care or group homes. The system that generates these numbers is underfunded in every function that would interrupt them.

**Institutional Protection (CIA / Epstein)**

The Kerry Committee (1989) documented the CIA providing political cover and transport infrastructure to drug trafficking networks. The structural model is identical whether the cargo is drugs or people. Epstein operated a trafficking network for decades. His 2006 Florida prosecution was shut down via a non-prosecution agreement. U.S. Attorney Alexander Acosta later stated he was told Epstein "belonged to intelligence" and to leave it alone. The full client list has not been released as of 2026.

**The Pattern**

Funding flows toward acquisition, away from accountability. Missing children consistently go unreported. Trafficking victims consistently come from state custody. Investigations into powerful networks are consistently stopped. Oversight is consistently under-resourced. This pattern holds across decades, administrations, party lines, and agencies. At what point does a sustained, consistent pattern cease to be a series of independent failures and become a system working exactly as designed? This report documents the pattern. The answer is left to the reader.
