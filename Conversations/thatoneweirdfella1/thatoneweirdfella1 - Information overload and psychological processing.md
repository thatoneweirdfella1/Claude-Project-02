**ASSISTANT:**
 That's called **cognitive overload** at the base level, but the more specific term for what you're describing is **information flooding** or **overwhelm induction**.

The deliberate version has a few names depending on context:

**Shock doctrine** (Naomi Klein's term) is the political macro version. Hit people while they're reeling from the last crisis before they can organize around it.

**Psychological flooding** is the clinical term for exposure to stimuli faster than the nervous system can regulate. In trauma therapy it's accidental. As a control mechanism it's intentional.

**Attentional capture cycling** is the more academic framing. Each new threat captures attention before the last threat is resolved, which means nothing ever gets full processing and nothing ever gets a full response.

The one that fits your 2023 angle most precisely is probably **crisis stacking** or **narrative saturation**. The idea being that if you stack enough legitimate-seeming crises, the audience loses the ability to distinguish signal from noise, prioritize, or sustain outrage long enough to act on any single thing.

There's also **learned helplessness** (Seligman) as the downstream psychological state that results from sustained inescapable stressors. That's the end condition you'd be engineering toward.

You looking for the psych framing to build an argument, or more for sourcing the research side of it?

**ASSISTANT:**
 ADHD brain. Almost certainly.

Caffeine works by blocking adenosine receptors. Adenosine is the chemical that makes you feel tired. Block it, most people feel alert.

ADHD brains are already running a dopamine deficit and have dysregulated arousal systems. So caffeine hits differently on the first exposure, sometimes dramatically, then the receptor dynamics shift fast. Some ADHD people get the crack effect once and then it flatlines. Some get paradoxical calm instead of stimulation. Some get anxiety with zero focus benefit.

The "within a year or two" thing is interesting though. That's way past tolerance. That sounds more like your baseline neurochemistry resetting to a state where the caffeine has something to actually work with again, and then one hit blows through whatever that window is and closes it again.

There's also a genetic angle. The CYP1A2 gene controls how fast you metabolize caffeine. Slow metabolizers get weird extended effects. Fast metabolizers barely feel it at all. Combined with ADHD receptor dynamics you can get really idiosyncratic responses that don't follow normal tolerance logic.

Short version: caffeine is a mild stimulant that works on a neurotypical arousal system. You don't have one of those. So it doesn't follow the rules.

**ASSISTANT:**
 Yeah that's the same mechanism confirming it.

Nicotine is a stimulant. Makes most people alert, focused, suppresses appetite. For a chunk of ADHD brains it does the opposite. Sedates. Same paradoxical response you get with caffeine, just more pronounced because nicotine hits acetylcholine and dopamine receptors more directly.

The pipe/less inhalation thing is smart instinct but the sedation isn't about quantity, it's about how your brain is responding to the compound itself. Less of it just means less tired instead of not tired.

This is actually part of why so many undiagnosed ADHD people end up heavy smokers historically. For some it genuinely does provide focus and they're self-medicating. For others they're chasing a buzz that only half-works and keeps requiring more to get there. Your version just skipped straight to "nope, sedation."

The pattern you're describing across caffeine and nicotine, both stimulants that sedate you instead, is pretty textbook paradoxical stimulant response. It's one of the cleaner informal indicators that the ADHD diagnosis is on the right track neurologically.
