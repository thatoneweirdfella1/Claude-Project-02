# Saint Peter at the pearly gates

*2026-06-10T04:26:10.134541Z*

---


**HUMAN:**
Who greets you at the pearly gates. I forget what people say his name is


**ASSISTANT:**
 Peter. Saint Peter.


**HUMAN:**
Was he a disciple.


**ASSISTANT:**
 Yes. One of the twelve. Fisherman from Galilee. Jesus called him first, before most of the others. Originally named Simon. Jesus renamed him Peter, which means "rock." Built the early church on him after the crucifixion. First pope in Catholic tradition.


**HUMAN:**
Okay. So clear this up for me. If you were a disciple, does that automatically make you a saint? And is that the same as an apostle? If not, what the hell is an apostle?


**ASSISTANT:**
 Good questions. Three different things, though they overlap a lot.

**Disciple** just means follower. Thousands of people were disciples of Jesus. It's a broad term. The twelve are the inner circle but disciple itself isn't a title, it's a relationship.

**Apostle** means "one who is sent." Specifically commissioned by Jesus to go out and spread the message. The twelve disciples became apostles after the resurrection when Jesus sent them out. Paul also called himself an apostle even though he never met Jesus in person, because he claimed a direct commission from the risen Christ. So apostle is a specific role with a specific job.

**Saint** is the complicated one. In Catholic and Orthodox tradition, saints are officially declared through a formal process called canonization. You need documented miracles, a life of exceptional holiness, the whole thing. So no, being a disciple doesn't automatically make you a saint in that sense.

BUT. In early Christianity and in Protestant tradition, "saint" just meant any believer. Paul addressed his letters to "the saints" in various cities, meaning the whole congregation. Regular people.

So the overlap: the twelve disciples became the twelve apostles. Most of them were later declared saints by the Catholic Church. But those are three separate designations with three separate meanings.

Peter hits all three. Disciple, apostle, saint.
