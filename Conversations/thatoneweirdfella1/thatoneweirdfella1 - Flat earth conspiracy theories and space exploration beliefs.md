**ASSISTANT:**
 The flat earth / moon landing split is worth breaking down separately because they're different claims with different consensus numbers.

**Flat earth** is genuinely fringe. Global polls put it around 2-3% who seriously doubt the earth is round. Some countries run higher (a few surveys in places like Turkey or Indonesia show 10-15%) but in Western countries it's basically noise.

**Moon landing doubt** is a completely different animal. That's not a fringe position at all. Depending on the country and how the question is asked, anywhere from 20-50% of people have doubts. A 2019 YouGov poll found about 1 in 6 Brits thought it was faked. Some polls in Russia and other countries run way higher. Americans hover around 10-20% depending on methodology.

So the guy saying "5% flat earthers" is probably in the right ballpark. Moon skepticism is a much bigger tent.

**"Been to space but not the moon"** specifically as a category -- that's not really a tracked consensus position but it's probably the most common form of moon skepticism. People accept ISS footage, satellites, Hubble, all of that. The specific objection is the 1960s moon missions. That's where the "we couldn't do it then" argument lives.

Biblical / young earth creationism is a separate axis entirely. US numbers on that are genuinely wild -- Gallup has consistently found around 40% of Americans hold some form of creationist belief. That's not fringe by any definition.

So your read is basically right. Flat earth is stupid AND small. Moon skepticism is much larger and the arguments are at least more coherent even if you disagree. Lumping them together is a bad faith move by both sides.
