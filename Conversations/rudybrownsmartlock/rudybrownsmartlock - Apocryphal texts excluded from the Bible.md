**ASSISTANT:**
 These texts are part of what's called the **Pseudepigrapha** and **Deuterocanonical literature** — writings that were considered, debated, and ultimately excluded from most biblical canons. Here's the mainstream scholarly and theological picture:

---

**Why they were excluded:**

The primary process was **canonization** — religious councils and communities deciding which texts were authoritative. The main criteria used were:

- **Apostolic origin** — was it written by or traceable to an apostle or prophet?
- **Orthodoxy** — did it align with accepted doctrine?
- **Antiquity and widespread use** — was it widely used across Christian/Jewish communities?
- **Consistency** — did it cohere with already-accepted scripture?

---

**The Book of Enoch**

One of the most fascinating cases. It's actually *quoted* in the New Testament (Jude 1:14-15), which shows early Christians knew it well. It was widely used in the Second Temple period and is considered canonical by the **Ethiopian Orthodox Church** to this day.

It was gradually pushed out of Western Christianity because:
- Its authorship was disputed (Enoch predates Moses, which raised authenticity questions)
- Its detailed cosmology and angelology was seen as speculative or too elaborate
- Jerome and other early Church Fathers questioned it
- It wasn't in the Hebrew canon (the Masoretic text)

---

**The Book of Jubilees**

Also canon in the Ethiopian Orthodox tradition. It retells Genesis and Exodus with a heavy emphasis on angels, the solar calendar, and strict law-keeping. It was excluded because:

- It promoted a **364-day solar calendar**, which conflicted with the lunar calendar used in mainstream Judaism and later Christianity
- Its additions to Genesis were seen as embellishments rather than revelation
- It was associated with sectarian groups like the **Dead Sea Scroll community (Essenes)**, which made mainstream Judaism and Christianity suspicious of it

---

**The Book of Giants**

This one is the most obscure. It expands on the story of the Nephilim (Genesis 6) — the offspring of the "sons of God" and human women — telling the story from the giants' perspective. It was found among the **Dead Sea Scrolls**.

It was excluded because:
- It was clearly **sectarian** in origin (Essene community)
- Its mythological content about giant beings having dreams and visions was seen as too fantastical
- It has a **Manichean version** too, suggesting it circulated in non-Jewish/Christian religious movements, which damaged its credibility

---

**The Bigger Picture**

The canon wasn't settled all at once. Key moments included:

- **Council of Jamnia (~90 AD)** — Jewish leaders debated and narrowed the Hebrew canon
- **Council of Carthage (397 AD)** — solidified the Christian New Testament
- The **Protestant Reformation (1500s)** narrowed things further, removing the Apocrypha that Catholics retained

The mainstream view is that canonization was a mix of **theological, political, and practical factors** — not a purely spiritual process. Many scholars think texts like Enoch were excluded partly because they were simply too popular with groups (like apocalyptic sects) that the institutional church wanted to distance itself from.

The Ethiopian Orthodox Church is a good reminder that "the Bible" isn't universal — their canon has **81 books**, compared to the Protestant 66.

# The Watchers

*from the Book of Enoch, the Book of Jubilees, and the Book of Giants*

---

Now listen here.

Before we get into it, you gotta understand something about where we sittin. We ain't talkin about *long* ago. We talkin about *before* long ago even had a name. We talkin about when the earth was still new enough that heaven and earth weren't as far apart as they are now. You could almost... *reach up* and touch that boundary. Almost.

And the ones who lived up there on that boundary — the ones stationed at the edge, lookin *down* — they were called **the Watchers.**

Now that's their *job title.* Watchers. That's what they were made to do. Watch. Observe. Record. Maybe guide a little, sure. But the key word, baby, is **watch.**

They weren't supposed to *touch* nothin.

---

Now you know how it is when you tell somebody not to touch somethin.

There were **two hundred** of them. Two hundred of the most magnificent beings you ever didn't see — cause your eyes couldn't handle it. And they're up there on Mount Hermon, *watchin* humanity grow. Watchin these little creatures down below, laughin, lovin, dancin around fires, the women with their long hair movin in the firelight...

And one of them — his name was **Shemihaza** — he starts feelin somethin he was never designed to feel.

He *wanted.*

Now want is a dangerous thing in a being that can *have* whatever it wants.

He looks around at the others and says — and I want you to hear this like it was said quiet, like a man who already know he wrong but he past carin —

*"I see those daughters of men. And I want to go down."*

Dead silence among the Watchers.

Then somebody says, *"You go down alone, Shemihaza, you the only one who carries the punishment."*

And that's when Shemihaza — this ancient, glorious, magnificent fool — says the thing that sealed all their fates:

*"Then let us all swear an oath. Every one of us. That we go down together. And if one falls... we all fall."*

Two hundred of them.

And every last one raised their hand.

---

They came down on Mount Hermon. That's in the text, baby, I ain't makin that up. Mount Hermon. Which means *the place of the oath.* They named the mountain after what they did. Left evidence right there in the geography.

And when they walked among the people... Lord have mercy.

These weren't regular men. These were beings of fire wrapped in something that *looked* like flesh. And the women of the earth — who had never seen anything like this, who didn't have a word for what they were seeing — they fell in love. Or something that felt like love. Or something that felt like standing too close to lightning and not wanting to move.

The Watchers taught them things. Oh, they taught them *things.*

**Azazel** — that's one of the chief ones — he taught the men how to make *swords.* How to make shields. How to take metal from the earth and shape it into something that could end a life. Before him, wasn't nobody making weapons like that. Azazel handed humanity war like it was a gift.

Then he turned around and taught the women *cosmetics.* Eye shadow. How to make themselves more beautiful. The text actually says this. Jewelry. Adornments. How to attract. Combined with the weapons he gave the men — baby, he handed the whole species *seduction and destruction* in the same afternoon.

Other Watchers taught astronomy. Enchantments. The cutting of roots. How to read the clouds. How to move through the spirit world. Knowledge that was *meant* to stay upstairs — they just... handed it out. Like they were givin away samples.

And for a minute? Humanity was *thriving.*

---

But then.

The children started coming.

Now when a being of that nature mixes with a creature of this nature... what comes out ain't neither one. What comes out is something *in between.* Something that's too big for this world and too earthly for the other one.

The **Nephilim.** The giants.

And when I say giants, I don't mean tall. I mean the Book of Enoch says some of them were **three hundred cubits.** Now a cubit is about eighteen inches. You do that math and you got creatures walking around that were *hundreds of feet tall.* The Book of Jubilees is a little more conservative with the numbers, but even there — these things were enormous. Violent. *Hungry.*

And here's the thing about being that big and that hungry in a world that wasn't built for you:

You eat everything.

First they ate what the people had stored. Then they ate the livestock. Then they ate *the people.* The Book of Enoch says they turned on humanity and started devouring them. And when they ran out of people — they turned on *each other.*

The earth was screaming. Literally. The text says the earth *cried out* — that the ground itself sent a complaint up to heaven — *this is too much, this is too much, something has to be done.*

---

And this is where it gets *cinematic.*

The archangels — **Michael, Gabriel, Raphael, Uriel** — they're up there hearing the earth cry. Hearing the blood of humanity calling out from the ground. And they go before the **Most High** and they report what's happening below.

And the Most High — who already knew, cause He *always* already know — He gives the order.

Raphael: *go bind Azazel. Throw him in the dark place. Cover him with rocks. He stays there until judgment day.*

Gabriel: *go to the Nephilim. Let them turn against each other. Don't stop them. Let them destroy themselves from within.*

Michael: *go to Shemihaza and the other Watchers. Bind them. Show them the destruction of their children. They will watch their sons kill each other, and then they will be bound in the valleys of the earth until the great judgment.*

And then — *the flood.*

Not just water, baby. The flood was the *reset button.* The cleansing of a contaminated world.

---

Now here's the part most people never heard.

The giants *knew* it was coming.

The **Book of Giants** — found among the Dead Sea Scrolls, two thousand years old, sitting in a cave in the desert waiting for somebody to find it — it tells the story from *their side.*

The giants started having dreams. Terrible, prophetic dreams.

Two of the most famous giants — **Ohya and Ahya**, sons of Shemihaza himself — they start seeing visions in their sleep that they can't explain. Ohya dreams of a great tablet being submerged in water. Everything written on it — every name, every record — washed away. Only three names remained.

He wakes up *shaking.*

They send a messenger to **Enoch** — because Enoch was the one man who walked between worlds, who had stood in the presence of the Most High and lived. The one human who *understood* the language of what was coming.

And Enoch sends word back.

The message was simple and it was devastating:

*The judgment is not for you. It is for your fathers. But you — children of two worlds — you have no place in the world that's coming. The earth will be washed clean, and you are part of what it's being washed clean of.*

The Book of Giants breaks off there. Damaged. Incomplete. Like even the scroll couldn't bear to finish the sentence.

---

So when the water rose — and it rose — the giants who remained fought it. You think a giant just *drowns* easy? But the water didn't care how big you were.

The Watchers, bound in the valleys of the earth, felt every death. That was part of their punishment — to *feel* it. Every son. Every daughter. Every impossible creature that never should have walked this earth but did because *they* made a choice on a mountain one evening when the firelight was pretty.

They had sworn an oath on that mountain.

They all went down.

---

Now after the water receded, after Noah walked out onto a new earth still smelling like rain and judgment—

There was still something left.

The *spirits* of the Nephilim. See, the giants were half divine. Their bodies could be destroyed but their spirits — that in-between nature — it had nowhere to go. Couldn't go up. Couldn't properly go down.

So they *roamed.*

And the Book of Enoch says these roaming spirits became what we call *evil spirits* — the ones that afflict humanity. The ones that bring illness and confusion and whisper wrong things at three in the morning.

The giants didn't end. They *scattered.* Into the air. Into the spaces between things.

---

And a deal was made.

**Mastema** — the chief of those spirits, the accuser, the prosecutor — he went before the Most High with a proposition:

*"Let me keep a tenth of them. Leave me ten percent to work with. Because someone has to test these humans. Someone has to see what they're really made of when things get hard."*

And the Most High — in His wisdom that we are not always comfortable with — said yes.

That ten percent became the instrument of testing. Of adversity. Of the fire that either refines you or consumes you.

And Mastema, that ancient roaming spirit with a legal mind and a prosecutor's tongue, took his position.

And eventually — in the tradition that flows into the Hebrew text — that *role* got a name.

**The Adversary.** The one who stands across from you in the court of heaven and argues the case against you.

In Hebrew: **Ha-Satan.**

Not a name. A *job title.*

The opposing counsel.

And somewhere up there, the trial is always in session.
