During those same ten years, Epstein built the most consequential social network of any private individual in modern American history. He moved through Harvard, through the White House social circuit, through the boardrooms of the largest financial institutions in the country. He flew politicians, scientists, lawyers, and royalty on his private aircraft. He cultivated access to virtually every center of power in the United States and several abroad.

Maria Farmer made her complaint in 1996. She told the FBI what Epstein was. The Bureau took the information and did nothing visible with it. Epstein, now apparently aware that a federal agency knew his name in a criminal context and had declined to act, continued building his network for a decade. The flight logs document who flew with him. The guest lists document who visited his properties. The ten-year gap between the first documented government knowledge and the first arrest is not a mystery waiting for explanation. The Architecture Above the Banks

JPMorgan does not operate in isolation. It operates within a financial architecture that connects the largest banks in the United States to the agencies that regulate money creation, the institutions that manage sovereign wealth, and the private forums where the people running all of these entities meet and coordinate outside of any democratic process.

Understanding that architecture requires starting with the Bank for International Settlements.

The BIS was founded in 1930, headquartered in Basel, Switzerland. It describes itself as a bank for central banks. Its legal status is unlike any other financial institution in the world. Under its founding agreements and subsequent host country treaties, the BIS and its senior management enjoy diplomatic immunity. Its property and assets are immune from seizure, expropriation, or government interference. Its archives have the same inviolability accorded to diplomatic missions. It pays no taxes in Switzerland or in the other jurisdictions where it operates. Its internal deliberations are not subject to judicial oversight by any national court.

The BIS serves as the primary coordinating body for the world's central banks. Governors of the Federal Reserve, the Bank of England, the European Central Bank, the Bank of Japan, and every other major central bank meet at the BIS on a regular basis to coordinate monetary policy. Those meetings are not public. The communiques that emerge from them are often vague. The substance of what is agreed is not reported in real time to the legislatures that are theoretically accountable for monetary policy in each of the participating countries.

The BIS has a documented history that does not appear in its promotional materials. During the Second World War, the bank continued to operate under its American president, Thomas McKittrick, a Harvard-educated banker with close ties to the German Reichsbank. Under McKittrick's oversight, the BIS received gold from the German Reichsbank that postwar investigations confirmed had been looted from the central banks of Belgium and the Netherlands. The BIS cooperated with postwar investigations and returned 3.7 tonnes of gold to the Allied Tripartite Commission in 1948. McKittrick left the BIS in 1946 and joined Chase National Bank, a predecessor institution to JPMorgan Chase, as a senior vice president and director. The Federal Reserve Bank of New York gave the BIS a clean bill of health in 1947. The institution continued without interruption.

The question the wartime history raises is not whether the BIS is currently engaged in comparable conduct. It is whether an institution that operated continuously through the Second World War while accepting looted gold and maintaining relationships with the central banking representatives of belligerent nations on both sides, and that then received a clean bill of health and continued operating with the same structural immunities, represents the kind of institution that should sit at the center of global monetary coordination without democratic oversight. That question has never been formally resolved. The institution simply continued.

The Federal Reserve Is Not Federal

The Federal Reserve System was created by Congress in 1913. Its Board of Governors is appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate. Its governors serve fourteen-year terms, deliberately staggered to outlast presidential administrations. It does not receive funding through congressional appropriations. It does not require congressional or presidential approval for its monetary policy decisions.

The twelve regional Federal Reserve Banks are incorporated similarly to private corporations. Their stock is owned by member banks, which are required to hold it as a condition of membership. JPMorgan Chase is among the member banks. The stock pays a fixed dividend. The member banks elect a portion of each regional bank's directors. The Board of Governors in Washington, appointed through the public political process, holds the remainder.

The Fed describes itself as independent within the government rather than independent of government. That distinction is meaningful in theory. In practice it means that the institution which controls the money supply of the United States, which sets the interest rates that determine the cost of every mortgage, every car loan, every business credit line in the country, operates on a decision-making timeline and with a personnel selection process that is substantially insulated from the elected officials who are accountable to voters.

This is not a secret. It is documented in the Federal Reserve's own publications and acknowledged by its own leadership. What is less often discussed publicly is what happens to the people who run the Federal Reserve when they leave.

In March 2020, at the onset of the COVID-19 economic crisis, the Federal Reserve awarded BlackRock a no-bid contract to manage three of its emergency corporate bond-buying programs. This was not the first time. During the 2008 financial crisis, the Federal Reserve gave BlackRock no-bid contracts to manage approximately one hundred and thirty billion dollars in distressed assets from Bear Stearns and American International Group. In both cases, BlackRock was allowed to purchase its own exchange-traded funds on behalf of the Federal Reserve, meaning the firm was simultaneously acting as the Fed's agent and as a seller of the products the Fed was buying. Senator Chuck Grassley raised this concern at a 2009 Senate Finance Committee hearing, asking why BlackRock was the only firm qualified to manage the assets of the special purpose vehicles. Treasury Secretary Timothy Geithner answered that they come with a world-class reputation.

In August 2019, several months before the COVID emergency, former BlackRock executives who had previously served at central banks published a paper called Going Direct, presented at the G7 summit of central bankers in Jackson Hole, Wyoming. The paper proposed blurring the lines between government fiscal policy and central bank monetary policy, arguing for direct central bank intervention in credit markets. When the COVID crisis arrived the following year, the Federal Reserve implemented the Going Direct framework. BlackRock was hired to execute it.

BlackRock manages approximately fourteen trillion dollars in assets under management as of the end of 2025. This figure is larger than the gross domestic product of every country on earth except the United States and China. BlackRock's founder and chairman, Larry Fink, has no elected mandate, no Senate confirmation, and no accountability to any public body. The firm he runs makes decisions about capital allocation that shape employment, inflation, housing costs, and industrial investment across the entire global economy.

The Group of Thirty is a private body of financiers and academics founded in 1978 with initial funding from the Rockefeller Foundation. Its membership consists of current and former heads of major central banks and private financial institutions. Current and former Federal Reserve chairs, presidents of the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, chairs of the Bank for International Settlements, and chiefs of the International Monetary Fund sit alongside the senior leadership of the world's largest private banks. Janet Yellen, who served as Federal Reserve Chair from 2014 to 2018 and as Treasury Secretary from 2021 to 2025, is a CFR member and co-chaired a Group of Thirty working group on climate finance. The Group of Thirty's deliberations are private. Its membership roster is public. The meetings themselves and what is agreed in them are not subject to any public accountability mechanism.

The BlackRock Layer

The 2020 COVID emergency crystallized a relationship that had been developing for over a decade. The Federal Reserve needed someone to execute an unprecedented bond-buying program. It did not go through a competitive bidding process. It called BlackRock. BlackRock executed the purchases, including purchases of its own products, with the Fed's money, with taxpayer money allocated through the CARES Act to absorb potential losses.

This arrangement did not emerge from a conspiracy. It emerged from a structure in which the same small network of individuals moves between the Federal Reserve, the Treasury, the major private banks, and the largest asset management firms, carrying institutional relationships and mutual trust that make formal competitive bidding processes feel unnecessary. The revolving door is not a metaphor in this context. It is the actual mechanism by which the boundaries between public monetary authority and private financial power have been made effectively invisible.

The people who run BlackRock, JPMorgan, and the Federal Reserve share overlapping board memberships, alumni networks from the same universities, histories of employment at each other's institutions, and membership in the same private forums. The Group of Thirty brings the heads of private banks and central banks into a shared private deliberative space. The Council on Foreign Relations, whose membership has included every Treasury Secretary and Federal Reserve Chair of recent memory, provides another. Neither forum is formally empowered to make policy. Neither forum's deliberations are public. Both forums create the kind of ongoing trust-based coordination between public and private actors that makes formal corruption unnecessary because everyone already knows what everyone else wants.
