## **KEY RESEARCH FINDINGS:**

### **1. IDENTITY-BASED MOTIVATION (Social Psychology)**
- **Van Zomeren et al. (2008)** - Social identity model of collective action
- **Finding:** People act when they feel:
  1. Group identification ("people like me")
  2. Perceived injustice (shared grievance)
  3. Efficacy belief ("I can actually do something")

### **2. THE EMPATHY-ACTION GAP (Behavioral Economics)**
- **Small & Loewenstein (2003)** - Identifiable victim effect
- **Finding:** People don't act on abstract problems. They act when:
  - They see themselves in the situation
  - The problem feels personally relevant
  - The action is concrete and achievable

### **3. DIFFUSION OF RESPONSIBILITY (Bystander Effect)**
- **Latané & Darley (1968)**
- **Finding:** People assume "someone else will do it"
- **Solution:** Direct address + specific ask + small first step

### **4. UNCERTAINTY REDUCTION THEORY (Communication)**
- **Berger & Calabrese (1975)**
- **Finding:** People won't act when outcome is unknown
- **Solution:** Normalize the ask, show it's been done before, reduce perceived risk

### **5. COMMITMENT & CONSISTENCY (Cialdini)**
- **Cialdini (2006)** - Influence principles
- **Finding:** Small initial commitment leads to larger ones
- **Solution:** "Foot in the door" - start with tiny ask that aligns with self-image

### **6. AUTHORITY THROUGH VULNERABILITY (Leadership)**
- **Brené Brown (2018)** - Dare to Lead
- **Finding:** Leaders who show vulnerability build trust faster than those who project invincibility
- **Why:** "If they understand my struggle, they understand the problem"

## **STUDIES ON CREDIBILITY & DOUBT:**

### **1. INOCULATION THEORY (McGuire, 1961)**
**Finding:** Preemptive weak attacks BUILD resistance to stronger future attacks

**Application:** 
- If YOU mention potential criticism first (weakly), audience develops antibodies
- When real attack comes, they've already processed and dismissed it

**BUT:** Only works if the preemptive mention is BRIEF and CONTROLLED

**Risk:** Detailed preemptive disclosure = planting seeds of doubt that weren't there

---

### **2. PRIMACY EFFECT (Information Processing)**
**Finding:** First information received has disproportionate impact on judgment

**Application:**
- If they hear about your past FROM YOU first = you control framing
- If they hear FROM ATTACKERS first = their framing sticks

**BUT:** This assumes they'll definitely find out

**Risk:** If they WOULDN'T have found out, you just told them yourself

---

### **3. SUSPICION TRIGGERS (Fein, 1996)**
**Finding:** Unprompted defensive statements CREATE suspicion where none existed

**Classic example:**
- "I'm not a crook" (Nixon) = made people think he was
- Mentioning you're "not asking for power" when no one suggested you were = plants the idea

**Application:**
- Don't defend against attacks that haven't happened yet
- Each defense = new question in audience's mind

---

### **4. TRUST & VULNERABILITY (Brené Brown, but backed by organizational psych)**
**Finding:** Appropriate vulnerability AFTER trust is established = strengthens bond
Vulnerability BEFORE trust = seen as oversharing/red flag

**Application:**
- Initial contact = competence + clarity
- After response/relationship = vulnerability strengthens
