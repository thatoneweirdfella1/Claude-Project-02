**Cognitive Dissonance** = The mental discomfort you feel when you hold two contradictory beliefs, values, or attitudes at the same time, or when your actions contradict your beliefs.

**Simple example:**
- You believe smoking is bad for you
- BUT you smoke cigarettes
- Your brain feels uncomfortable holding both = cognitive dissonance

**Real-world examples:**
- "I care about the environment" but drives a gas-guzzler
- "I value honesty" but lies to avoid conflict
- "All people deserve rights" but supports policies that deny rights to certain groups

**What happens:**
Your brain tries to resolve the discomfort by:
1. Changing your belief
2. Changing your behavior
3. Justifying/rationalizing the contradiction
4. Ignoring the contradiction

**Example from psychology:**
After making a difficult decision, people convince themselves it was the RIGHT decision to reduce dissonance (even if evidence suggests otherwise).

**Why it matters:**
- Explains why people resist facts that contradict their beliefs
- Why people double down when confronted with evidence they're wrong
- How manipulation works (create dissonance, then offer resolution)

That's cognitive dissonance. It's a psychological defense mechanism your brain uses to maintain internal consistency.
