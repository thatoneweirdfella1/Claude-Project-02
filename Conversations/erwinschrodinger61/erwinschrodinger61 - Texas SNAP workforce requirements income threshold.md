If you're **not** hitting that threshold, recipients required to work must work at least 20 hours per week or 80 hours per month, and document their hours to continue receiving benefits — submitting them to the Texas Health and Human Services Commission for verification.

A few other exemptions that get you out of the workforce referral regardless of income:
- Caring for a child under 6
- Physical or mental health condition preventing work
- Going to school or a training program at least half-time
- Already receiving or applying for unemployment benefits

Worth noting: Texas has a "full family sanction" policy where if the primary caretaker can't meet the 80 hours/month requirement, the entire household can be removed from SNAP for up to six months — so the stakes are pretty high.
