1. Material compatibility - The pump's internal materials need to be compatible with water. If it's designed for oil, the seals and gaskets might degrade in water over time.

**Why an oil pump isn't ideal for water:**

1. **Seal & gasket materials** - Oil pumps use seals designed for petroleum products. Water can degrade rubber seals and gaskets over time, causing leaks and failure.

2. **Lubrication** - Oil lubricates the pump's internal moving parts. Water has no lubricating properties, which increases wear on bearings and mechanical components, reducing pump life.

3. **Viscosity mismatch** - The pump is designed for oil's thickness. Water's lower viscosity can affect flow performance and potentially cause seal slippage.

4. **Corrosion risk** - Water promotes rust and corrosion in metal parts not specifically treated for water exposure.

**Bottom line:** 
- **Short-term/emergency**: Probably okay for brief use
- **Regular/long-term**: Not recommended—consider a dedicated water pump instead

If you only need it occasionally for water, it may work, but expect reduced lifespan and potential seal degradation. For regular water pumping, a centrifugal pump or submersible pump designed for water would be much more suitable and reliable.
