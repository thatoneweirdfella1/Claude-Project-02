**HERZL'S EXPLICIT REJECTION OF RELIGIOUS FRAMING**

In his private diary, Herzl wrote: "I am completely irreligious and do not feel compelled to hold on to religion for its own sake."

Herzl was a secular, cosmopolitan intellectual. He lacked Hebrew, Yiddish, and Russian — the languages of practicing Jewish communities — and was a doctor of law and minor playwright by background.

His foundational text, *Der Judenstaat* (The Jewish State, 1896), framed Zionism entirely as a political solution to antisemitism, not a fulfillment of biblical prophecy.

**HERZL'S VIEW OF JERUSALEM (Diary, 1898)**

After visiting Jerusalem in 1898, Herzl wrote:

"When I remember thee in days to come, O Jerusalem, it will not be with pleasure. The musty deposits of 2,000 years of inhumanity, intolerance, and uncleanliness lie in the foul-smelling alleys... This is not the language of someone operating from biblical mandate.

**HERZL'S STRATEGIC USE OF RELIGIOUS LANGUAGE**
