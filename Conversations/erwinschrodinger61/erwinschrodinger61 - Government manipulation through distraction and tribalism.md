 Define all the psychological terms listed.**Tribalism** — The instinct to protect and believe your group, and distrust anything outside it. Truth becomes secondary to loyalty.

**Confirmation bias** — You unconsciously seek, favor, and remember information that agrees with what you already believe, while dismissing what challenges it.

**Cognitive dissonance** — The discomfort of holding two contradicting beliefs at once. The mind resolves it not by finding truth, but by rationalizing whichever belief is more comfortable.

**Illusory truth effect** — Repetition alone makes something feel true. Hear it enough times, it stops feeling like a claim and starts feeling like a fact.

**Moral licensing** — Doing one "good" thing gives your brain permission to stop being vigilant. Sharing one exposé makes you feel like an informed person, so you stop digging.

**Outgroup homogeneity bias** — You see your own group as complex individuals, but the opposing group as one undifferentiated mass. "They're all the same." It shuts down nuance before it starts.

**Reactive devaluation** — If the wrong person says something, you reject it automatically. The source poisons the content before you've even evaluated it.

**Naive realism** — The quiet assumption that you see reality as it actually is, and people who disagree are either uninformed, irrational, or biased. Never you.

**The backfire effect** — When confronted with hard evidence against a deeply held belief, some people don't update. They dig in harder. The correction strengthens the original belief.

**Dunning-Kruger** — The less someone actually knows about something, the more confident they feel about it. Competence introduces doubt. Ignorance feels like clarity.

**Retrospective determinism** — Once something happens, it feels like it was always inevitable. This makes it easy to assign blame to past generations without accounting for what those events actually looked like in real time, before the outcome was known.

**Audience capture** — You start performing for the crowd watching you. Over time, the crowd's expectations reshape what you actually believe. You stop leading your own thinking.

**Social proof** — If everyone around you believes something, your brain registers that as evidence it's true. Not logic. Just headcount.

**Manufactured consent** — The process by which institutions shape the boundaries of acceptable thought so thoroughly that people police their own beliefs without any force required. You censor yourself.

**Normalization creep** — Radical things become acceptable through gradual exposure. Each step seems small. The distance traveled is enormous.

**Authority bias** — Credentials, titles, and institutional backing cause the brain to suspend critical evaluation. You outsource your judgment to whoever sounds official.

**The Overton window** — The narrow range of ideas that feel socially acceptable to discuss at any given time. Everything outside it feels fringe or dangerous, not because it is, but because the window was moved.

**Emotional reasoning** — If something *feels* true, the brain treats it *as* true. Strong emotion becomes a substitute for evidence.

**Telescoping bias** — Recent events feel more significant and vivid than distant ones. Makes the present feel uniquely urgent, and history feel less real and relevant than it was to the people living it.

**Ingroup signaling** — Beliefs become badges. You adopt positions not because you've reasoned to them, but because holding them identifies you as part of the right group.

**Availability heuristic** — Whatever comes to mind most easily feels most likely or most important. Whoever controls what's visible controls what feels real.

**Identity-protective cognition** — When a belief is tied to who you are, evidence against it becomes a threat to your identity, not just your opinion. The mind defends it like survival.

**Pluralistic ignorance** — Everyone privately doubts the official story, but assumes everyone else believes it, so nobody speaks. The silence reads as consensus. The consensus was never real.

**The third-person effect** — The unshakeable feeling that propaganda works on other people, but not on you. It is the capstone. The finishing move. Because it doesn't just make you vulnerable — it makes you *proudly* vulnerable, convinced you're the exception, while every mechanism listed above runs quietly in the background, unchecked, exactly as intended.
