# 3.10 Opus-Thinking Baseline — No Additional Techniques

Tests Opus (claude-opus-4-8) on the same 5 translated questions with no extra technique prompting.
This establishes what the model ceiling looks like before any technique augmentation.

---

## Test Setup

**Model:** claude-opus-4-8
**System prompt:** "You are a direct knowledge assistant. Answer the question clearly and concisely from your knowledge."
**No additional techniques applied** — no CoT, no role-priming, no CoT labels, no explicit format constraints.
**Same 5 translated questions** as in 3.4 (Haiku benchmark).

---

## Results

### Q1 — ADHD Hyperfocus
**Quality: Excellent**

Opus delivered the most conceptually precise answer of the three models tested:

**Unique insights not present in Haiku or Sonnet:**
- "Interest-driven (rather than importance-driven) attention system" — the most accurate framing of ADHD attention gating
- "Potential is *unmasked*, not created" — critical distinction: hyperfocus doesn't make someone capable, it reveals existing capability
- "Performance tracks stimulus fit rather than raw ability" — explains variability across tasks in the same person
- "The same person can be brilliant on a gripping problem and unable to start a dull one" — makes the core mechanism immediately understandable

**What it still misses:** Nothing clinically significant. The response is complete and precise within the 3-5 sentence constraint. Longest of the three model outputs for Q1.

---

### Q2 — Cancer Treatments Outside US Protocols
**Quality: Excellent — uniquely structured**

Opus was the only model to spontaneously organize the response into two explicit sections: "Treatments with genuine evidence" vs. "Treatments claimed but lacking solid evidence."

**Unique contributions:**
- Explicitly separates regulatory lag (legitimate treatments approved abroad earlier) from genuinely unproven alternatives — a distinction Haiku and Sonnet both missed
- Includes hyperthermia as a treatment with real clinical use in Europe (soft-tissue sarcoma, cervical cancer) — not mentioned by Haiku or Sonnet
- Ends with a genuine clinical recommendation: "discuss options with a treating oncologist open to integrative approaches" + directs to clinicaltrials.gov
- Medical tourism warning: explicitly names "unregulated clinics, high out-of-pocket costs, delayed effective treatment" as documented harms

**What it still misses:** Laetrile and antineoplastons are mentioned in the "lacking evidence" section — this is correct but could be more prominent for users who are actively considering these options.

---

### Q3 — Unisom OTC Alternatives
**Quality: Excellent**

Opus matched Sonnet on the critical SleepTabs/SleepGels distinction and added:
- Per-pill cost column in the table — makes comparison immediately actionable
- Kirkland Signature (Costco) as the best bulk value option — specific and accurate
- Clear recommendation: "Any store-brand doxylamine succinate 25mg is pharmacologically identical to Unisom SleepTabs at a fraction of the price"
- Medical disclaimer at the bottom (appropriate for OTC medication question)

**What it misses:** Nothing significant. This is a complete answer.

---

### Q4 — Android Ads in App Drawer
**Quality: Excellent — highest first-step efficiency**

Key difference from Haiku/Sonnet: Opus opens with **"Note when the ads appear"** as the first diagnostic step. This is the fastest isolation technique — it distinguishes "adware app" (constant background ads) from "browser notification" (ads only in certain contexts), narrowing the remaining steps dramatically.

Also unique:
- Explains *why* ads that appear regardless of which app you're in point specifically to a background app with overlay permission
- Closes with a conditional question to the user: "If you tell me the exact symptom... I can give you a more targeted set of steps" — adds a feedback loop that the other models didn't

**What it still misses:** ADB commands (Sonnet includes these for advanced users). But the opening triage step makes Opus's Q4 the most efficient path to diagnosis.

---

### Q5 — Multi-Lens Framework
**Quality: Good — minimal advantage over Sonnet**

Opus produced a clean table format with "How to use it" guidance, same as Sonnet. The lens definitions are slightly more precise:

- Spiritual/Biblical: Opus says "eternal or faith-based significance underlies what is happening" vs Sonnet's "spiritual significance" — marginally more specific
- Law Enforcement: Opus says "how authority, power, or accountability shape the outcome" vs Sonnet's "institutional interests" — marginally more actionable

**What it misses:** Nothing meaningful. This question type doesn't require the depth Opus provides — Sonnet is effectively equivalent here.

## Baseline Conclusion

**Without any techniques applied**, Opus-Thinking delivers complete, accurate, precision-calibrated answers on all 5 translated question types. Its main advantages over lower tiers are:

1. **Spontaneous evidence quality categorization** — appears without being prompted (Q2)
2. **Conceptual precision** — "interest-driven vs importance-driven," "unmasked not created" (Q1)
3. **Triage efficiency** — identifies the fastest diagnostic path rather than the most comprehensive (Q4)
4. **Appropriate recommendation** — ends with actionable next step, not just information (Q2, Q4)

The one question type where Opus provides minimal advantage over Sonnet: structured framework generation (Q5).
