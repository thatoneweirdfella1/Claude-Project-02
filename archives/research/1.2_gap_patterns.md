# 1.2 Gap Patterns — When I Say / I Actually Mean

Derived from the 10 examples in `conversation_examples_rambling.md`.

Each statement maps the surface behavior to the underlying intent.

---

## Pattern Statements

**Example 1 — ADHD Hyperfocus**

> When I narrate my personal history and self-discovery story before asking, I actually mean "give me a brief, science-backed explanation of this specific phenomenon as it applies to me."

**Example 2 — Coincidences in the 30s**

> When I reference a number or fact using only pronouns and no context ("what number did it say"), I actually mean "retrieve a specific figure from our previous conversation that I remember approximately but can't re-state precisely."

**Example 3 — Categorizing Events Through Multiple Perspectives**

> When I list the lenses I want something analyzed through in fragments and mid-sentence pivots, I actually mean "build me a structured categorization framework using exactly these analytical dimensions."

**Example 4 — Celebrity Appearance / Body Double Theories**

> When I wrap my question in investigative roleplay, authority framing, or "god-mode" language, I actually mean "take this claim seriously and analyze it rigorously — I want evidence and logic, not dismissal."

**Example 5 — ADHD-Friendly App Organizer**

> When I interrupt myself mid-sentence and mix technical requirements with ADHD-style formatting signals, I actually mean "these fragmented bursts are the actual specs — build exactly what I described, formatted for how I think."

**Example 6 — Alternative Cancer Treatments**

> When I open with a conspiracy framework as if it's settled fact, I actually mean "I want real information about this topic, viewed through this lens — meet me where I am, not where conventional sources are."

**Example 7 — Multi-Boot USB (Context Restoration)**

> When I paste a one-sided conversation and say "you can get a gist," I actually mean "read this, reconstruct the project, and continue from where we left off — I don't want to re-explain everything."

**Example 8 — Cheap Alternatives to Unisom**

> When I describe a product through autocorrected noise ("unison hat"), I actually mean "you know what I'm talking about — figure it out and answer the underlying question anyway."

**Example 9 — Barrel Connector Wire Polarity**

> When I say "will this work" and attach an image without explanation, I actually mean "verify my specific setup and tell me if I've done it right — the image is the full context."

**Example 10 — Apps Menu Flooded with Ads**

> When I open with pure frustration ("Wtf is this"), I actually mean "something broke recently and I need it fixed — skip the explanation of what's happening and go straight to the fix."
