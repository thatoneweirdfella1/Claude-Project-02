## PART 1: USER GUIDE

### What This App Does

You ask questions. The app translates what you actually meant, picks the right AI model, and gives you a better answer than you'd get asking raw.

**It doesn't fix your writing.** It doesn't judge. It translates ADHD thinking patterns into AI-readable questions.

---

### How It Works (The Journey)

**You type your question** (rambling, emotional, unclear — that's fine):
```
"so ive been thinking about the architecture problem but also i notice the logging 
is weird and do the models handle like edge cases for the thing, and also im 
overthinking this but which model should i even use"
```

**The app translates it** (breaks it down, clears the fog):
```
Translation: You're asking three separate questions:
1. How should architecture handle the logging issue?
2. Which model best handles edge cases for your specific use case?
3. Should you pick based on problem characteristics or cost/time constraints?

Confidence: 84%
```

You can accept, edit, or ask the app to try again.

**The app picks a model** (fast for simple questions, powerful for complex ones):
```
Routing:
Q1 → Opus-Fast (architectural analysis, moderate complexity)
Q2 → Haiku (straightforward comparison)
Q3 → Haiku (decision logic, low complexity)

Confidence: 81%
```

You can override if you want.

**The app adds techniques** (tricks to get better answers):
```
Selected: Chain-of-Thought (for reasoning), Explicit Scope (for focus), 
Quote-First (for grounding)

Confidence: 79%
```

You can add/remove if you disagree.

**You get answers** (one per question):
```
Q1: Route the logging fix and architecture question to Opus-Fast because that's 
a structural decision, not a lookup. The edge-case comparison and "which model" 
decision are both fast questions. Splitting them is what fixes the overthinking.
```

**You rate it** (thumbs up/down or comment):
```
Did this help? ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4 stars)
Comment: "Good but I wanted more on the logging part"
```

The app learns from your feedback. Over time, it gets better at your question types.

### What Each Part of the Screen Means

**Title Bar:** Shows the app name and connection status (Connected, Syncing, Error).

**Ribbon (Top Navigation):**
- **Compose:** The main screen (what you're looking at now)
- **History:** See all your past questions and answers
- **Settings:** Change defaults (model preference, feedback style, etc.)
- **Techniques:** Browse the 18 techniques and how they work

**Your Input Card:**
- **Where you type:** Your raw question, as rambling as you want
- **Edit button:** Change it after translation if you want
- **Translate button:** Starts the whole process

**Translation Results Card:**
- **The translated question(s):** What the app thinks you actually meant (one sentence each)
- **Confidence percentage:** How sure the app is (80%+ = very sure, 60-79% = pretty sure, <60% = needs clarification)
- **List of translated questions:** Each one gets routed separately

**Techniques Card:**
- **Technique pills:** Show which prompt tricks the app applied
- **Color coding:** Green = active technique
- **What they do:** Each technique is explained in the Techniques reference screen

**Answer Card:**
- **The actual answer:** One per question, in order
- **Feedback buttons:** 👍 = helpful, 👎 = not helpful, 💬 = add comment

**Scenario 1: You Get a Perfect Answer**

1. Click on the Techniques reference screen
2. Find T02 (Chain-of-Thought) or whichever one you're curious about
3. Read: What it does, when it helps, examples
4. Go back to your answer

## PART 2: GLOSSARY

**Ambiguity:** How unclear the question is. "Should I apply for this job?" is high ambiguity (depends on many factors you haven't stated). "What's the capital of France?" is low ambiguity.

**Chain-of-Thought (T02):** A technique that makes the AI show its reasoning step-by-step instead of jumping to conclusions. Helps when the question needs explanation, not just a fact.

**Complexity:** How many reasoning steps are needed. "Fix a git merge conflict" is complexity 3. "Design a system architecture" is complexity 8.

**Composed Prompt:** The final prompt that gets sent to the model. It's the translation + selected techniques + output format, all combined.

**Confidence:** How sure the app is about its decision. 80%+ = proceed automatically. 60-79% = show result but ask for confirmation. <60% = ask clarifying questions.

**Consequence of Error:** How bad it would be if the app got this wrong. Choosing a sleep aid has high consequence (health). Picking a coffee maker has low consequence (worst case: bad coffee).

**Haiku:** The fastest, smallest Claude model. Use it for quick facts, comparisons, straightforward how-tos. Fastest = cheapest.

**Hallucination:** When the AI makes up information that sounds right but isn't. Example: Inventing a feature that doesn't exist in a product. The app tries to prevent this with certain techniques.

**Instruction Hierarchy:** A technique that tells the app what's most important. Instead of trying to do everything equally, focus on #1 first, then #2, then #3.

**Novelty:** How unusual or new the question is. "How do I boil water?" is low novelty (standard question). "How do I design an ADHD-specific AI routing system?" is high novelty.

**Opus-Fast:** The balanced Claude model. Use it for most questions. Faster than Opus-Thinking, more capable than Haiku.

**Opus-Thinking:** The deepest Claude model. Use it for complex reasoning, novel problems, high-stakes decisions. Slowest = most expensive, but can handle hard questions.

**Override:** When you decide the app's choice (translation, model, techniques) isn't right, you click a button to change it. The app respects your override.

**Prompt:** The full instruction sent to the AI model. It includes the question, the techniques, the output format, and constraints.

**Rambling Input:** Your raw question, potentially tangential, emotional, unclear, or multi-part. This is what the app is designed to handle.

**Routing:** The app's decision about which model to use. Haiku for simple, Opus-Fast for medium, Opus-Thinking for complex.

**Scope:** How broad or narrow the question is. "Should I move?" is broad (many factors, many answers possible). "Is New York's weather good on June 22?" is narrow.

**Technique:** A prompt engineering trick that improves AI answers. The app has 18 of them (T01-T18). Each one does something different (add reasoning, reduce hallucination, clarify scope, etc.).

**Tempo (Translation, Execution, Model, Prompt, Output):** Not an app term, but TEMPO = the 5 stages the app runs through. You're on the user-facing side; the app handles it.

**Threshold:** A cutoff point. Confidence threshold = the percentage the app needs to reach before it proceeds automatically vs. asking clarification.

**Token:** A unit of text. The longer your prompt, the more tokens it uses, and the more expensive (and slower) it is. Haiku uses fewer tokens than Opus-Thinking.

**Transparency Mode:** The app shows you every decision: what it translated, which model it picked, what techniques it selected, what the final prompt is. You can see everything and override anything.

**Translation:** The app's interpretation of what you actually meant. "My sleep aid stopped working, why?" instead of "I took unisom for years and suddenly..."

**Validation:** A check that the composed prompt is correct before sending it to the model. It ensures no contradictions, no missing format specs, no over-complexity.

## PART 3: FAQ

### GENERAL

**Q: Why does the app exist?**
A: Because ADHD thinking patterns (tangential, emotional, multi-part questions) don't map cleanly to how AI models expect questions. The app translates between them.

**Q: Who is this for?**
A: Anyone who thinks by talking/writing first, figuring it out second. Not exclusive to ADHD, but designed for that pattern.

**Q: Do I have to use all the features?**
A: No. If you just want answers, click Translate and you're done. The overrides and techniques reference are there if you want them.

**Q: Is my data private?**
A: Yes. The app doesn't train on your questions. Your feedback is logged locally for the learning system, not sent to Anthropic.

---

### TRANSLATION

**Q: What if the translation is wrong?**
A: Click Edit and retype what you meant. Or ask the app to "try again." The confidence score tells you how sure the app is.

**Q: How does the app know what I mean if I'm not clear?**
A: It looks for patterns: buried requests, tangential context, emotional language, pronouns that don't resolve. It's good at untangling ADHD-style rambling.

**Q: What if I ask five questions at once?**
A: The app will split them into five separate questions and route each one separately. It detects compound requests automatically.

**Q: Can I ask it NOT to translate?**
A: If your question is already clear, you can set "Skip translation" in Settings. But the default is on for good reason.

---

### ROUTING

**Q: Why did it pick Haiku instead of Opus-Thinking?**
A: Because your question was straightforward (low complexity, no ambiguity, clear answer). Haiku is faster and cheaper for those.

**Q: When should I override to a more powerful model?**
A: If the question is complex, ambiguous, or high-stakes, and the answer matters. Or if you just want deeper reasoning for the sake of it.

**Q: What if I always want Opus-Thinking?**
A: You can set that in Settings (Default Model). But it's slower and more expensive. The routing algorithm already optimizes for "use the smallest model that works."

**Q: Can I use Haiku even for hard questions?**
A: Yes, the override is always available. But you might get a surface-level answer. The app picked a bigger model for a reason.

---

### TECHNIQUES

**Q: What are techniques?**
A: Prompt engineering tricks that improve AI answers. Examples: "Show your reasoning step-by-step" (Chain-of-Thought), "Ground in specific examples" (Quote-First), "What are you assuming?" (Assumption Surfacing).

**Q: Why does the app pick certain techniques?**
A: It analyzes what the question needs (accuracy? clarity? bias protection?) and picks techniques that address those needs.

**Q: Can I add my own techniques?**
A: Not in this version. But you can skip techniques if you don't want them, or override in the future to ask the app to add a specific one.

**Q: What's the difference between T02 and T11?**
A: T02 (Chain-of-Thought) is "show your work step-by-step." T11 (Reasoning Decomposition) is "break this into smaller components first, then reason about each." Similar but different flavors.

---

### ANSWERS

**Q: Why is this answer so long?**
A: Because the question was complex or ambiguous, and the app selected techniques that prompt for detailed reasoning.

**Q: Why is this answer so short?**
A: Because the question was straightforward (simple lookup or narrow decision), and Haiku was sufficient.

**Q: Can I ask a follow-up?**
A: Yes. The next question you ask will see your previous answer in the conversation history (optional). Or ask fresh.

**Q: What if the answer is completely wrong?**
A: Rate it 1 star and comment what went wrong. The app logs this and learns. If it's consistently wrong for a question type, the app will eventually change its routing or techniques for that type.

---

### FEEDBACK

**Q: Does the app learn from my feedback?**
A: Yes. After 15-20 questions of the same type, the app detects patterns. If a question type consistently gets low ratings with one model, it tries a different model or technique for similar questions in the future.

**Q: How long does learning take?**
A: ~15-20 questions per pattern. So if you ask 5 product research questions, the app will start learning about product research questions by Q20.

**Q: Can I see what the app learned?**
A: Yes, in the History screen, there's a "Patterns" tab showing which question types are trending toward which models.

**Q: Can I turn off learning?**
A: Yes, in Settings. But the app gets better with time, so keeping it on is recommended.

---

### SETTINGS

**Q: What can I customize?**
A: Default model preference, confidence thresholds, feedback style (explicit vs. auto-infer), technique limits, data controls.

**Q: What does "Explicit Confidence Threshold" mean?**
A: If your confidence threshold is set to 75%, the app won't proceed to routing unless translation confidence is 75%+. Default is 80%.

**Q: Can I delete my history?**
A: Yes, in Settings → Data Controls → Clear History. Or selectively delete individual interactions.

---

### TROUBLESHOOTING

**Q: The translation is always wrong for my question type.**
A: This is rare but happens. Try: (1) Rephrase your question slightly, (2) Check if you're being too terse or too rambling, (3) Report in Settings → Feedback.

**Q: The model keeps refusing to answer.**
A: Some questions hit safety guidelines (illegal content, violence, etc.). If you think it's a false positive, reframe and try again.

**Q: The app is slow.**
A: If it routed to Opus-Thinking, that's expected (deep reasoning takes time). If Haiku is slow, it's a network issue.

**Q: I want to understand why it picked that model.**
A: Click the "Routing Details" link in the routing card to see the decision tree trace.

---

### ADVANCED

**Q: Can I see the final composed prompt?**
A: Yes, click "Show Prompt" in the Composition card. This is for power users who want to debug.

**Q: Can I edit the final prompt before sending to the model?**
A: Not in this version. But you can override techniques before composition.

**Q: How do I know if a technique actually helped?**
A: Compare ratings across similar questions with/without that technique. Or check the Pattern Analysis in History.

**Q: What's the learning algorithm doing?**
A: After you rate answers, the app groups interactions by question type. If a pattern emerges (e.g., "product research questions get low ratings with Haiku"), it recommends changing the routing rule. Manual review is needed before applying.

## PART 5: TIPS & TRICKS

**Tip 1: Use Translation as a Clarity Tool**
Even if you think your question is clear, look at the translation. It often surfaces assumptions you didn't realize you were making.

**Tip 2: Star Your Best Answers**
Give 5 stars to answers that actually helped. The app learns from this and gets better.

**Tip 3: Comment on Bad Answers**
Don't just rate low. Tell the app what was wrong: "Good but I needed specifics," "Way too technical," "Missed the main point." This is the learning signal.

**Tip 4: Use Override Sparingly**
The routing algorithm is usually right. Override only when you know the app made a mistake or you want to experiment.

**Tip 5: Check History Regularly**
After 20-30 questions, look at History → Patterns to see what's working for you. The app will tell you.

**Tip 6: Set Your Style in Settings**
If you prefer concise answers, set feedback style to "Minimize verbosity." The app learns over time.

**Tip 7: Pair with Another Tool**
This app translates questions well. But for research, add a search engine. For code, add an IDE. It's one tool in your toolbox.

## PART 6: WHEN TO USE, WHEN NOT TO USE

**Good use cases:**
- Clarifying a rambling or ambiguous question
- Deciding between options (which model, which approach, which tool)
- Understanding a complex concept (the app will break it down)
- Getting feedback on your idea (the app's questions surface unstated assumptions)
- Learning a new skill (step-by-step guidance)

**Not ideal for:**
- Very simple factual lookups ("What's the capital of France?") — ask directly
- Sensitive personal details you don't want logged — the app logs feedback for learning
- Real-time conversations — each question is independent
- Legal/medical/financial advice that needs professional judgment — the app is a thinking tool, not a advisor
